# The world

World generation, terrain, biomes, roads, buildings and what stands in them, and the World Generator page. These notes were CLAUDE.md's; search `src/js` for the names in them. Keep them up to date with the code, with targeted edits.

- **The World Generator** (`game/WorldGen/`, served at `/WorldGen/`, and `/test/WorldGen/` from test): a hand-written page, not built from `src/`. `gen-worker.js` fetches `../index.html`, evaluates its script with stand-ins for three.js and the page (as the tests do), and reads the world through the names in its `NAMES` list (`settlementLat`, `settlementAt`, `seaV`, `SEA_T`, `seaPlot`, `coastType`, `plotType1`, `superOf`, `zoneAt`, `setName`, `setKind`, `PLACE_KIND`, `BIGP`, `bigCell`, `portCell`, `cvSite`, `capitolPlan`, `climateAt`, `bioPick`, `BIOMES`, `reliefNat`, `realmOf`, `rlfSnow`, `rlfRock`, `rlfValleys`, `plainsH`, `riversNearBox`, `MAPC`, `TEST_GROUPS`, `wondersIn`, `coastKind`, `WD_NAME`). The land comes in levels (`land(tx,tz,L)`: 256 plots a side times 2^L, sampled every 2^(L+1)) from a second worker of its own, so it zooms out to 0.01 pixels a plot; settlements, bases and rivers are only asked for and drawn from `SITES` (0.1) in. It needs the script to keep ending in `requestAnimationFrame(frame);\n})();` (as the tests do) and the save key to start `goblin-city-v`. Renaming one of those names means updating the list. Its Land & sea, Land shape and Climate sliders set `window.GSG_WORLD` (or the older `GSG_SEA`) in the worker before the script runs, which `worldCfgSet` puts in `WORLD_CFG` (`world/coast.js`, defaults `WORLD_DEF`: `land`, `size`, `rough`, `chan`, `isles`, `islz`, the climate's `temp`, `rain`, `czone`, `lat`, the land shape's `mtn`, `mth`, `hill`, `lakes`, and the rivers' `riv`, `rmd`, `rwd`, `rwid`, `rlen`, `rbr`, read in `world/routes.js`, where `rivCfg` sets `RIV_W`, `RIV_JR`, `RIV_LEN` and `RIV_TRIB`); the game never sets it, so its worlds stay as they are, but a save keeps any setting that isn't a default (`cfg`, from `worldCfgSent`, read back in `load`), so a guest online builds the host's world with them. Its Climate chips draw `climateAt`'s temperature or moisture, or the biomes (`bioPick`, coloured by each one's `map`), over the land (`world/climate.js`, op `clim`), or the Height layer (`reliefNat` out in the wilds, with rock, snow and lakes, `hg`/`hk`, shaded in `climPic`). Hill shading and Contours (Show chips) draw from the same tiles (`shadePic`, `contours`, heights smoothed by `hSmooth`); the hover box asks op `pt` (`point`: `plainsH`, rock, snow, the biome). Its 3D button (`v3Open`, state `V3`) lays the map's middle square, drawn as it is into a texture, over heights from op `grid` (`grid`: `reliefNat` in the wilds, 0 in towns, a shallow sea bed; valleys only within 48 blocks a sample), with three.js r128 from cdnjs fetched on first use (`v3Lib`); drag turns and tilts, the wheel or a pinch goes nearer, Height scales the heights (`v3Ex`). Its View's Noise (`NZ`, `setView`, address `noise=<field>`) draws only the raw fields (`NZF`: land, t, m, bio, rug, h, riv, town) from the worker's op `noise` (no roads, rivers' courses or chunks; `init` with `lite` skips the capitol), in passes a sample every 16, 8, 4 and 2 pixels (`nzGo`), each a layer at its own place (`nzRender`); a slider sends op `cfg` (`worldCfgSet`, the settlement caches cleared) and redraws, with no reload; back to Map loads the game again only if the settings changed meanwhile, in the same workers (`start` keeps a worker that's loaded it, `W.ok`; the worker keeps the file, `HTML`, and `loadGame` empties its stand-in page and holds back the game's timers and listeners, so nothing of the old game stays alive), as do a new seed and the Map's sliders: `cfg` alone isn't enough there, since `worldCfgSet` doesn't clear every cache the map reads (`coastC`). Rivers marks where rivers and streams rise (op `rsrc`: nodes with `rivWet` and no `rivInflow`, at `rivX`) from `RSRC_SC` in; Towns is `dn*4` plus the chance (`dn` as `settlementLat` steps places down); Height far out asks right on the climate's lattice corners, where `climAt` works out just the one corner. The river and town fields repeat `rivBias`/`rivSrcM` and `setNone`'s sums: change them together. `tools/ui/wgnoise.js` tests it and times it against the map. Its Wonders and Coasts layers draw `detail`'s `wd` (diamonds, named zoomed in) and `cf` (each shore plot's `coastKind`) with key entries; `window.__wg` (`go`, `det`) is for `tools/ui/wgcoast.js`.
- **Settlements in `WORLD_CFG` (New world's and the World Generator's Settlements group):** `towns` (0.2 to 1: the share of grid points with a place, `setNone`), `csize` (city and large town radius, capped at 30), `clu` (below 0 shrinks the nudge off the grid point toward none; above 0 empties grid points where `nzNoise` over the grid, salt 4481, is low) and `sites` (multiplies `farmCand`'s and `bigCell`'s odds). An empty grid point is a place with `S.none` (type village, R 0): `settlementRaw` skips it and `edgeOn` builds no highway to it, as for the sea; the start's (0,0) is never empty. Only grid points and `hash2`/`nzNoise`, nothing about the land. Each default leaves the number exactly as before (worlddet unchanged). The seed line adds "custom" when `worldCfgSent()` has anything. Suite `setcfg` (`CFG=` the world it boots).
- **Territories (`world/territories.js`, maps `ui/map-territories.js`):** the land is split into territories over a lattice of tiles `TER_N` (24) cells a side, a tile `6*WORLD_CFG.ter` settlement grid points across (`terSt`: `TP` grid points, `TT` plots a tile, `cs` plots a cell; 6912 blocks a tile at the default). Each tile may have one seed, its biggest land place (`terSeed`, dropped now and then by `tvar`, with a head start `off`), kept only if it wins at least two places (`terSeedOK`). Ownership is a multi-source Dijkstra over the tile and its 8 neighbours (`terRun`), each step's cost from the terrain sampled per cell (`terSamp`: `seaV`, `reliefNat` with valleys off, `ruggedAt`, lakes, rivers from the `rvIdx` buckets): sea, lakes, coastlines, rivers, high and rugged ground cost more, so borders follow them. `terTile` caches it, `terAt(px,pz)`/`terOfSet(S)` answer for a plot or place, `terInfo(id)` names it (`NM_A` plus `TER_SUF`, stepped along the lattice so neighbours never share one; never a nearby place's name) and gives its sovereignty (`TER_SOV`, by weight) and `title`. Per-frame code only reads tiles already worked out (`terLook`, `terPeekCell`), warming the rest a few milliseconds a frame (`terWarmTile`, `terWarm`): the pause map (`mapTerDraw`), the minimap (`miniTerDraw`) and the banner (`terBannerTick`). Settings `ter` (Size) and `tvar` (Variety) in `WORLD_CFG` (New world's and the World Generator's Territories group); they change nothing else in the world. Only `hash2`, no `Math.random`. The World Generator's op `ter` sends a tile, its layer `ter` and Noise field `ter`. Suite `terr` (determinism in any order, places per territory, names, the world unchanged, save and load); `tools/ui/wgter.js` the World Generator, `tools/ui/mapter.js` the pause map and banner.
- **New world's seed (`seedFromText`, `core/new-city.js`):** the `#nwSeed` box in the New world panel (`#confirm`): blank is random, a whole number is that seed, other text is FNV-1a over its characters (`Math.imul`, mod 1e9). It's read only when the panel is open (the Testing Environment's resets stay random) and handed to `freshWorld(off,tenv,cfg,sd)`. The World Generator's `toSeed` is the same function written out again: change both together. Suite `seedin` checks they agree.
- **Landscape noise (`world/noise.js`, plan in `docs/biomes-plan.md`):** `nzNoise` (2-D gradient noise), `nzFbm`, `nzRidged`, `nzWarp` (into `NZ_W`), `nzSmooth`/`nzClamp01`, all arithmetic only (no `Math.sin`/`cos`/`tanh`/`exp`/`random`), seeded through `hash2` with a salt per use; new landscape code should use them, not `gridNoise` or trig. `climateAt(x,z)` (temperature and moisture, 0..1, into `CLIM`; a weak north-south swing `latBand`, the start's region always temperate within `START_R0`) is in `world/climate.js`; `realmOf` (sea, city or plains, `world/coast.js`) was `biomeOf`. Suite `worlddet` prints chunk hashes for seeds 1, 2, 3 (they change only when world generation's meant to) and checks the noise repeats exactly and uses arithmetic only, then each biome's share of the land and a chunk of each, and the open plains' heights.
- **Biomes (`world/climate.js`):** every biome is a record in `BIOMES` (index `BIO_I`; its ground `top`/`sub`, `grass`/`leaf` tints, `map` colour, `trees` by species, scattered and wood odds `sc`/`dn`, `wood` and `pond` biases, `bush`/`flower`/`rock`, `gravel`, `reeds`, `ice`), picked from temperature rows by moisture columns in `BIO_TAB` (`bioPick`; badlands are desert where a patch noise says). The climate is worked out once per corner of a `CL_SP` (64) lattice (`clCorner`, `CL_C`) and blended (`climAt`, then `clBlend('pond'|'wood')` for the smooth fields); `biomeAt(x,z)` nudges each column's climate by `bioJit` (a patch noise about 24 blocks across plus a little per column, at most `BIO_J`, and the badlands' patch noise too), so two biomes meet in a broad band of patches; `clCorner`'s smooth fields (`gr`, `lf`, `pond`, `wood`, `dune`, `mesa`) are each biome's share of the climates those nudges reach, so tints and landforms fade across that whole band; `coastFlav` uses the same nudge, and a headland fades in with ruggedness (`natLand`: low and set back behind the beach at first); the far view's `farBio` takes the nudged biome and `bioTint` per plot (`tools/ui/bordershot.js` takes real screenshots of the nearest borders and coasts, `T3=` another build); `biomeOfPlot` is a plot's for the maps and the far view, `bioTint(x,z,g)` the grass (0) or leaf (1) tint, which `buildChunk` multiplies into the vertex colours of `GRASS` tops (half on its sides), `LEAVES` and `PINE` (grassland's is 1,1,1). Where kinds of ground meet (`G_MIX`: grass, dirt, sand, red sand, snow, mud, gravel, terracotta, salt, stone, sandstone), `buildChunk` blurs each column's top ground colour (each tile's average colour as painted, `TILE_AVG` in `core/atlas.js`, times the grass tint; nothing where the top isn't ground) over the chunk and `GM_M` (26) columns round it, a 25-wide box each way twice (`GM_S`/`GM_T` kept between chunks; other chunks' columns from `gmCols`, kept per chunk array in `GM_CC`, so meshing a chunk makes the chunks two out; where every column it reaches is one and the same colour, `gmSig`'s `u` on each array, it's left out and the meshes come out exactly as without it), and `gMix` gives each sky-facing top corner the blurred colour of its four columns over its own tile's average, capped at 1.45, so one ground fades into the next over about 50 blocks. The plains' columns (`plainsColumn`) take the biome's ground, ponds and plants, and `plainsTree` gives [ground, trunk, species] (`TREE_K`), grown by `treeGrow`; the far view keeps the species (`farTreesOf`, five numbers a tree) and colours the plains' ground and leaves by biome (`FAR_BIO`, `farBio`). New blocks: `SNOW`, `SANDSTONE`, `RED_SAND`, `TERRA`/`TERRA2`, `MUD`, `CACTUS`, `PINE`, `REEDS` (atlas tiles 96 to 106). A save carries `gen` (`WORLD_GEN`) and one from another generation isn't loaded.
- **Rivers (`world/routes.js`):** a drainage graph on a lattice of nodes `RIV_SP` (1600) apart (`rivNode`, its `f` from `seaV`): each land node drains to the neighbour one step nearer the sea (`rivDs`, Manhattan steps to the nearest sea node, `rivDown`), so every river reaches the sea. A river rises at a node (`rivSrcM`, likelier in rugged or, low down, wet ground, `rivBias`, never in or by a settlement, `rivSrcOK`, which mustn't ask anything about farms or open plains or generation goes round in circles) no more than `RIV_LEN` steps from the sea, and is a main river (`rivMain`) from there down; tributaries (`rivSrcT`, up to `RIV_TRIB` steps long, `rivTu`) run down until they meet one (`rivTo`). `rivWet` says which an edge is (2 river, 1 stream). An edge is drawn along the old tile curves (`rivFull`, `rivAt`; `rivGuide`/`rivOff` as before), cut where it crosses the next row or column of nodes (`rivX`) and joined into runs per tile (`rivRuns`, `rivTile`), each a route with `kind:'river'`, `w` (0.6 to 1 of `RIV_W` per river, `rivWide`: seeded by the node where it meets the sea; a stream 0.6 of its river's) and `trib`; `plainsRiverColumn` makes the banks from the biome's ground (`x`,`z` passed in; mud per 16 blocks of bank where the undithered biome is swamp). Lakes aren't faded out by rivers (`roadNear`'s `RN_N` leaves rivers out, for the lakes' fade in `plainsH`), so a lake can reach a river: there `plainsRiverColumn` (given riverCol's answer, with `cx`,`cz` the nearest point on the middle line and `tx`,`tz` its direction) runs the bed on under the lake, puts the banks under water where a lake's within 4.5, and raises the river's water to the lake's level, `rivLvl` (G where a bank's within 4.5 of a lake, then a block lower each `RIV_STEP` (10) along the line, looked for 20 either way: a steady slope, no weirs); `rivH` is plainsH remembered round rivers (`RIV_H`, `RIV_LV`, emptied by `worldCfgSet` and `clearWorld`). The maps leave out a river's pieces over the sea (`seaV`), and a source lake `R.src` [x,z,r] where a river rises from one (`riverCol` returns `lake`, `plainsRiverColumn` leaves it alone, `rlfSrcLake` sinks it in `reliefNat`; `roadNear` and `plotNearLines` keep the flattening and placements off it). Valleys: `reliefNat` multiplies the mountains by `rlfValley` (bilinear over a 16-block lattice, `rlfVCorner`, from the rivers within `RLF_VR`, `rlfVList`: floor `RLF_VM`, width `RLF_VT` per class); `rlfValleys(false)` turns valleys and source lakes off (the World Generator does, zoomed out). Suite `worlddet` hashes it.
- **The plan mustn't depend on the order it's asked in.** Many lookups keep their last answer in shared variables (`roadNear`'s `RN_RT`/`RN_O`/`RN_N`, `RLF`, `CLIM`), and asking about a block for the first time can set off a great deal else being worked out, another column's `plainsH` included, which overwrites them. So take what you need from such an answer into locals straight after asking, before asking anything else (as `plainsH` does), and never cache an answer worked out while something it depends on is only half done (`themeBusy`, `apBusy`). Suite `siteorder` asks every plot of a square in four orders, from nothing each time, and makes chunks in two; every line should say 0 different.
- **Relief (`world/relief.js`, plan sections 4.2 and 5):** the datum rule: everything built (towns, farms, sites, roads, railways, rivers) stays at G; only open plains (`PLAINS_OPEN`) rise. `reliefNat(x,z)` is the land's own height above G, its parts in `RLF` (`hill` from `plainsRaw` times `WORLD_CFG.hill`; `mtn` from ruggedness, `ruggedAt` on the climate's lattice as `clCorner`'s `r`, through `rlfTop` (`RLF_TOP`, at most `RLF_MAX`) and warped `nzRidged`; `lake`, low wet ground below G, at most `RLF_LAKE`). `plainsH` (`world/country-sites.js`) scales hills and lakes toward roads and plot edges as before, and the mountains through `reliefMask`: each thing nearby lets them rise only so steeply (roads and railways 1 a block, past their verge, from `rlfLines16`/`rlfDist` and `railDist` since `routeAt`/`railAt` don't look far enough; rivers 0.7; natural shores 1.2; farm lanes, links and truck stops 1; airports 0.12 and airfields 0.25 out, `rlfAir`, so their planes' fixed heights stay clear), setting `RLF.cut` (cut back: a bare rock face) or, over a road or railway with more than `RLF_TUN` (plus a motorway's deck) of land above, `RLF.tun` (left standing: `routeColumn` makes a concrete-lined, lit tunnel wherever the block at G+12 is solid, walls open where something joins or a railway crosses; `railPass` clears its own bed through it). `plainsColumn` makes lakes (ice in the ice fields), bare rock above `rlfRock(t)` and snow above `rlfSnow(t)` (lower the colder), `plainsTree` grows nothing there or on cut faces, and `biomeOfPlot` says `alpine` for high rugged plots. The far view draws it with `farTerrain` (boxes per ring, `plainsH` near, `reliefNat` further), the maps shade it (`reliefMapCells`, from `plainsH`, cached per plot in `RLF_MAPC`), and anything moving over chunks not made yet asks `landTop` (an AI plane in `aiFly` keeps 18 over it, `S.clr`). A lake deeper than about 3 isn't faded out for a highway (`roadNear` leaves the highways' part in `RN_RT` and the rest in `RN_O` for `plainsH`): it runs under, and `routeColumn` lays a deck on pillars over the water (`lk`). A railway under the land gets a lined, lit tunnel in `railPass`. `plainsColumn` adds scree (gravel, cobble) in the 12 blocks below `rlfRock` and two-block boulders where `nzNoise` (salt 4441) says at the mountains' feet. Lakes stay out of the start's region (`START_R0`/`START_R1`, as `ruggedAt`). Places in rugged country are smaller (`settlementLat` steps the type down by `ruggedAt` at the lattice point). Big blasts carve each column from its own ground (`bbGround`, plainsH per column, kept in the blast's `K.gm`). Beside what's built (`reliefMask`'s edges) the mountains rise in terraces (`RLF.ter`), 2 high and 4 deep for the first 48 blocks out and 3 high after, from any block within four that isn't open (`rlfShut`, cached per plot in `RLF_SH`, cleared with `plotCache`), each with a cobble retaining wall on its edge column (`RLF.tw`, laid in `plainsColumn`), rather than a cut face. `bigCell` turns sites down in rugged country (`ruggedAt` over 0.5, none from 0.65). Sites sit at G, so none goes where the mountains' part of the land (mountains, mesas, escarpments) round it stands high: `rlfLotOK(px,pz,w,h,lim)` samples `rlfRoughAt` (that part's height from the climate lattice's corners, no valleys, leaving `CLIM`, `CL_Q`, `RLF` and `RLF_MS` as they were, since it's asked mid-`plainsH`) five points a plot over the lot and a plot round it, against `RLF_SITE` (9; mines and quarries 18, the ski slope never asked), from `bigCell`, `farmK`, `bunkerAt`, `dumpSite` and `airportOf`. The salt flats' levelling eases in with the salt's share (`nzSmooth(0.3,0.7,sa)`) rather than at a half. A wind turbine is never on farmland (`turbineAt` asks `farmlandOf`: the fields were laid there, no tower was built, and the rotor fell on loading). The debug Info pane's Biome line is `infoBiome` (`biomeAt`, Alpine on the bare rock), its Place line `whereLabel`. Only arithmetic and `Math.sqrt` (`rlfLen`, never `Math.hypot`), which `worlddet` checks.
- **Weather by region (`render/weather.js`, plan phase 4):** `WX.type` is the world's spell and `WX.k` its eased values, as before (the same for everyone online, `clockState`); what it is at a place is `wxLocal(x,z,y)` (`wxLocalOf`: by the climate there and the biome's record, `WX_SEAS` the season's shift, `WX_COLD`/`WX_WARM` where rain and snow swap, cold too within 25 of the snow line; the spell's own seed `wxSeed` from `WX.until`; remembered per 64 blocks in `WX_LC`), pure, so host and guest agree. The regional kinds (`reg` in `WX_TYPES`: `sand`, `dust` with `devil`, `haze`; keys `dust` and `haze` in `WX_KEYS`) are never picked for the whole world, but the debug menu can. Each player eases `p.wxk` toward their own (`wxPlayers`, `p.wxL`); `WXK` is the weather being drawn (the first player's, and each view's own inside `renderView`, which now wraps `renderView0`): the sky's light, clouds, fog (tinted by `dust`/`haze`), rain, snow and the sandstorm's `dustM`, and the weather's sounds. Dust devils are `DEVILS` (`devilTick`): a funnel of `DEVIL_N` boxes each in their own instanced mesh (`devilM`, at most `DEVIL_MAX`), with a skirt of puffs (parts of kind 2). Lightning strikes near a player in a storm; rain douses fires where it's raining. Snow lying: bands by temperature (`WX.snowP`, `WX.snowC`, `WX.snowCover` the temperate one as before, `WX.snowW`; `wxBands`, centres `WX_BAND_T`), plus `PERMA_SNOW` (tundra and ice fields, all year); `snowAt(x,z,y)` on the CPU (cars, goblins, vehicles, fires, setRaw's snowBorn, big blasts), and on the graphics card `snowHere` (`SNOW_GLSL`, meshing.js and the far city) from the cold map `COLD`/`COLD_TEX` (16-block cells wrapped every 4096: temperature lowered on the mountains by `coldLapse`, wetness `COLD_WET`, permanent snow; filled round each player by `coldTick`, nearest first). `chunkFreeze` freezes water in winter only where `climAt` is under 0.64. `SNOWB_U` goes to the shaders; the bands go online in the clock (`snowB`). Suite `wxregion`; `tools/ui/wxshot.js` real screenshots.
- **Living in each biome (plan phase 4):** a place's biome is its middle's (`townBio`): `BIO_TOWN` (`world/climate.js`, on each record as `B.town`) gives the step down in size (`dn`, read in `settlementLat`), house and shop walls (`w`, `sw`) and how steep house roofs rise (`rk`: `b.roofK`, `b.roofN` the steps, read by `houseRoof`). Goblins' clothes by biome: `dressGoblin(g,true)` from `spawnCiv`/`spawnGoblin` calls `lookBiome` (`BIO_WEAR`, `WEAR_C`; accessories parka, hood, wrap, robe, reedhat, safari in `goblinModel`, roles `wear` and `trim`), not in the Testing Environment or a ship's pocket. The banner (`PB.bio`, `placeBannerTick`) names a biome held 3 s out in the open, and a place's banner says its biome. Biome sites (`world/biome-sites.js`): kinds `derricks`, `peat`, `research`, `safari`, `stilts`, `ski` at the end of `BIGP`, `BIO_SITES`, names `BIO_SITE_N`, columns `bioSiteColumn`, buildings in `city-plan.js`, props in `SITE_FN`; `siteBioW` weighs every kind by the biome at its middle (`BIO_SITEW`; the biome sites 0 outside their own, the ski slope by temperature and ruggedness), and `bigCell` lets mines and quarries into more rugged country than bases, launch sites and airfields. `farmCand` has no farms in the ice fields. A town's small shops and eateries are built its biome's way (`B.town.shop`: 'adobe', 'timber' or 'stone'; walls `shw` or `SHOP_W`): `bioShops` (end of `plotInfo`) sets `b.wall` and `b.bstyle`, which `roofOf` turns into a roof (`adobe`: parapet, corners and front stepped up, `LOG` viga ends; timber a false front or gable; stone a gable), a brand painted its own colour keeping its look. Street blocks lean its way too (`baseType0` over `baseTypeT`): `mk` more rows of shops, `pz` parks paved as plazas (below 0, plazas left as parks). `tools/ui/townshot.js` real screenshots. All these ask `bioNear(x,z)` (the biome of the nearest climate lattice corner, `clCorner`), never `climAt`: they're asked while a column's `plainsH`/`reliefNat` is mid-way through using `CLIM` and `CL_Q`, and `worlddet`'s relief repeat diff stops being 0. Suite `biomelife`; `tools/ui/biomeshot.js` real screenshots.
- **Wonders (`world/wonders.js`, plan phase 4):** one of each per lattice cell at most, worked out once into `WD_C` (`wdCell`/`wdIn`, like `rlfCell`) and built by `wonderColumn` (end of `genChunk`, after `sinkColumn`): stone arches (`wdArchAt`, `ARCH_SP`, desert, badlands and scrub, `archColumn`), waterfalls (`wdFallAt`, `FALL_SP`: a drop of 10 or more in `plainsH` found every 16 blocks and refined, `fallColumn`), landmarks (`wdLmAt`, `LM_SP`: `ribs`, `tree`, `baobab`, `erratic`, `henge` by biome, `lmColumn`). `wdBio` is a cell's biome only where the climate lattice's four corners round it agree (`clCorner`, never `climAt`, as in Living in each biome). `wonderNear`/`WD_NAME` name them in `whereLabel`; `wondersIn(x0,z0,x1,z1,ms)` lists them in a box for the maps ([kind,x,z], a fall at its lip; cells not yet worked out are left once `ms` is spent, `WD_MORE` says so). A fall's plunge pool is 6 deep under it, shelving to 3. In the sea: `seaStack` (from `seaWater` off a shore with `CF_RC` over 0.4, `stackOf`, `STK_SP`) stacks and arches. Fjords: a cold (`coastFlav` 1 or 2) shore with `CF_RC` over 0.5 stands its headland 16 to 40 high and sheer, and `seaWater` deepens the water under it (`fk`, faded in by ruggedness and out by 40 blocks; `oceanDepth` must run first, it sets `seaIsl`). Glaciers: `plainsColumn` lays `PACKICE` with crevasses on cold rugged ground within 14 of the snow line where `nzNoise` (salt 4451) says. `farPonds` draws a tarn at its own level for rings of step 8 or less. Suite `wonders`; `tools/ui/wondershot.js` real screenshots.
- **Coasts by biome (`world/coast.js`, plan phase 4):** `coastFlav(x,z)` gives a natural shore's flavour from the blended climate (dithered per column): 0 sand, 1 shingle (cold, `t` under 0.36), 2 an ice shelf (under 0.17), 3 mangroves (warm and wet), 4 the badlands' red sand; and sets `CF_RC`, how rugged the land is. `natLand` builds a headland where `CF_RC` is over 0.15 (out to `CF_HW`, 22 blocks in, as high as `reliefNat` times the ruggedness, at most 28; kept back from roads, railways, rivers and anything built by `coastCap`), else the old noise cliffs, mud and `mangroveCol` trees, shingle (snow over it when frozen) or sand; `seaWater` drops the bed faster under a rugged shore, keeps mangrove water 2 deep over mud (out to `CF_MW`) and lays the ice shelf (`iceShelf`, block `PACKICE`, ICE's look but never thawed, out to 11 to 24 blocks, floes beyond, never off a town's beach or harbour). The far view colours the shore by flavour (`FARC_CF`). `coastKind(x,z)` is the maps' kind (the five flavours, 5 cliffs and headlands, 6 fjords; `COAST_KC` colours, `COAST_KN` names): the pause map's `seaMapCells` colours natural shore cells by it (legend keys `cf0`..`cf6`) and draws wonders (`WD_LEG`, `wd_*`) over explored plots. A natural shore plot rises like open plains (`SHORE_OPEN`, `LAND_OPEN` in `world/country-sites.js`): `plainsH` fades it to G only within 14 of the water, and the distance to it (`RLF_SW`) is one of `reliefMask`'s edges, so the land behind a headland climbs into the hills. Suite `coastbio`; `tools/ui/coastshot.js` real screenshots.
- **Landforms (`world/relief.js`, plan phase 3):** `clCorner` carries how much of each a corner's biome has (`dune` desert, `mesa` badlands 1 and scrub 0.5, `salt` the driest desert), blended in `reliefNat`. Dunes (`rlfDune`: ridges across one wind per world, `RLF_DW`, slip face on the lee, 3 to 12 high) go on the hills' part (`RLF.dune`; sand all through in `plainsColumn`); mesas (`rlfMesa`: tables 18 to 40 high in steps of 5, canyons where `nzRidged` crests, `RLF_MS` flattening the hills on top) and escarpments (`rlfCliff`: ruggedness 0.3 to 0.6, up to 14, `RLF.face` making the face bare rock) on the mountains' part, so `reliefMask` cuts them back from what's built; salt flats (`RLF.salt`) level the ground to a `SALT` crust (block 162, atlas tile 107) with nothing growing. Tarns, oases and sinkholes are at most one each per lattice cell (`TARN_SP`, `OAS_SP`, `SINK_SP`; `rlfCell`/`rlfInCell`, worked out once into `RLF_TC`, the middle 64 blocks inside the cell so all of it's in the cell): only `plainsH` asks `reliefNat(x,z,true)` for them (feat), so far-and-wide callers (`biomeOfPlot`, the far rings, the World Generator) never set them working out. A tarn (`rlfTarnAt`: ruggedness over 0.55, open plains 3 plots each way, no road within 40 or river within 70; its level the lowest of its rim's natural height, `RLF_TN` while asking) sinks a bowl into the mountains' part, builds the rim up, and sets `RLF.wl`, the water's height above G, which `plainsColumn` fills over gravel (ice in the cold) and `plainsTree` keeps trees out of. An oasis (`rlfOasisAt`, in the desert, not salt) is a lake on the lake part with `RLF.oas` making a ring of grassland round it, dunes fading toward it, and palms (`TREE_K` 'palm', `rlfOasisD` in `plainsTree`). A sinkhole (`rlfSinkAt`, `sinkColumn` from `genChunk` after `plainsColumn`; none round the start or in the ice) is a shaft down to `bot`, undercut, water at the bottom where it's warm (`wet`). `tools/ui/crossdet.js` hashes a chunk of each landform too.

- **World and terrain:**
  - chunk generation `genChunk`; meshing (`meshes`, `MESHED`)
  - far LOD rings `farJobNear`/`farJobMid`/`farJobFar` via `farBox` (`render/far-city.js`, `updateFar`). Each job walks the plots of its ring and draws each through `farCached`, which keeps what a plot came to (`L.cache`, packed boxes) so a rebuild only works out plots new to it; a kept plot is redone when the ring's epoch moves on (`L.ep`: settings, a collapse, a crater, a blast under way, another world; the far ring also on any damage) or, in the near and middle rings, when blocks in it were smashed (`farEd`, over the whole superblock or country site). A build under way is never restarted because the player moved (that starved every ring at plane speeds), only when the settings change; a fast player's build is centred ahead of them and reaches further (`farVel`, `L.dur`, `FAR_M`/`FAR_MX`/`FAR_DUR`). `farPlan` goes over a place's streets a millisecond at a time before a plot near it is drawn (as `planAhead`, both through `planAsk`), so `setConns` doesn't stall a frame. A fourth entry in `LODS` is the backdrop (`farJobBack`, `farCellBack`): one box per 64 blocks (128 past a 3,200 view) from the climate, the sea, the kind of place and the land's height only, never the plan, built with a wide margin and first in the frame's budget (`FAR_BUDGET` 4 ms, `FAR_BUDGET_FAST` 6 past `FAR_FAST` blocks a second, `FAR_BACK_MS` while the backdrop itself doesn't reach); its shader (`FAR_BACK`) draws only where the ring whose band a spot is in hasn't been built that far (`L.cov`, set in `farCommit`), and each ring draws only inside what its shown build reaches, so the two never overlap. Suite `farspeed` flies at 20 to 600 blocks a second and fails if a ring or the backdrop is left behind; `tools/ui/farfastshot.js` photographs it flying and after a jump. Still a stall of up to a tenth of a second: the first question about a city's airport (`airportOf`), once per city; out on the plains, farmland and farm fields as patches and farms' and country sites' buildings come from `farCountry` (a landmark as columns of its real height, `farProfile`); trees there come from the same rules the chunks grow them by (`plainsTree`, `parkTree`, kept per block in `farTreesOf`), woods further off from `farWoods`, ponds and lakes from `farPonds`/`farTownWater`; a biome site's ground and what stands on it are sampled from its own columns (`farBioSite`, `farBioRuns`: runs of solid blocks every 2, 4 or 8 blocks by ring, kept in `FAR_BS`; `tools/ui/farsiteshot.js`)
  - world curve `CURVE_U`, lighting `WORLD_LIGHT`. Any second renderer (e.g. the obituary preview) must zero/whiten these while it renders.
- **Roads:**
  - towns `placeAt`; road spans `axisSpan` (kinds include `'moto'`, `'country'`; `flat` ends)
  - motorway ramps `hwRampAt` / `hwRampCol` / `rampBlocked` / `hwFootNear`; route ends `rtFlat`, `rtRamp`
- **Props:** furniture made of small boxes rather than whole blocks, in proportion to the goblins (about 1.4 tall): models in `PROPS` (from `src/assets/props/`, `propUnfight` run on each), placed by `hospProps`, drawn by `refreshProps` as instanced boxes near the player, knocked down by `knockProps`. Hospitals are laid out by hand; everywhere else `furnProps` reads what `furnRaw` says stands there, and `furnTurns` decides which of those blocks become props (shelves, fridges, sofas, beds, plants, small tables, lone screens). Restaurants (`restProps`), shops and stores (`shopRoom`), malls (`mallProps`), houses (`homeRoom`), flats (`aptFloor`), workplaces (`workRoom`) , public buildings (`civicRoom`) and venues and workplaces such as cinemas and factories (`venueRoom`) are laid out by hand from a shared set of models. Pavement and park benches and litter bins are props too (`streetFurn`, drawn by `refreshProps` out to 96 blocks rather than 34, and listed in `activeFix` so traffic hits them); taller street objects are fixtures (`drawFix`). Goblins treat props as walls through `gobGround`, and shove one over if held up for long. Suite `dispatch` sends out fire engines, police cars, ambulances and news vans from real stations. News crews (`reportNews`, `newsAI`) cover fires, car crashes and killings; suite `newscrew` checks each. Suite `proptest` chases a goblin across rooms; `tools/ui/proptime.js` times the props. Outdoor sites are dressed by `siteProps`, drawn by the same street loop out to 96 blocks: the plot types in `SITE_PROPS` (farmer's markets, camps, campsites, the cult, building sites, nurseries, trailer parks, cemeteries, mines, quarries, lumber camps, preppers, militia, thieves, ruins, dumps, parks, plazas, courts, lighthouses, houses), the big sites in `SITE_SUPER` (power stations, zoos, theme parks, stadiums, car parks, big parks), and bunkers and wrecked cars out on the plains (`siteKind` says which a block is; the newer ones are laid out in `SITE_FN`, bunkers' rooms in `bunkerProps`); suite `propaudit` counts props per kind of structure, so the bare ones show up. Campfires (`campFires`) are real fires in `hearths` (`hearthBurn`): they don't spread, burn out, call fire engines or put out in the rain, and only hurt someone standing right on the wood. Campsite motorhomes (vehicle `rv`) are a pool of their own (`RVS`, `manageRVs`), parked on their pitches out of sight, and can be wrecked like any parked car. Suite `campfire` checks the fire's reach, goblins and cultists by it, and the motorhomes.
- **Stairs:** `autoStair` gives any building with a real upper storey a flight, if it fits. A building with `b.stair` gets a railed flight per storey (`stairAt`) of stair blocks (`stairId`: a ramp underfoot, drawn as steps), and goblins chase a hippo between floors with `stairPoint`/`onStair`. Fire stations and schools so far; suite `stairs` tests going up and down. A very long building can have a second flight in `b.stair2` (read through `stair2Of`; so far just the university library).
- **Placement:** `SITE_F` says how far out from a place's middle each kind of site goes (`siteFits`); a dump is a 4x4 `dumpSite` at the end of a street.
- **Roofs:** `roofOf` picks a shape per kind of shop (hip, mansard, gable, barrel, false front, dome, mirror ball), drawn by `roofColumn` above the flat roof.
