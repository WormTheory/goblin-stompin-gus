Version 0.5

Mobile Gear (Testing Environment)

  • A new vehicle to try out in the Testing Environment: the Mobile Gear, an armoured walker eight blocks tall. Pick it from the vehicle buttons in the Testing Environment tab and it's set down in front of you. Only Gnash and Grizzle can pilot it for now.
  • Walk up and press F (controller Y) to climb in. Hold F for half a second to climb out, so a stray tap won't throw you out mid-fight.
  • It moves where the camera points, with the view from behind and above it so the crosshair sits in the middle of the screen and you can see over its head. On foot it sets off slowly and builds up into a jog, its strides lengthening and its arms swinging, stepping whichever way you're going, backwards and sideways included. Standing still, its legs stay planted as you look around until its body has turned too far on them, then they step round to catch up. Press C (controller B) to switch boost on and it drops into a wide, braced crouch and skates along the ground three times as fast, at no cost. Everything is about a fifth faster than it was.
  • Shift (controller X) is a quick boost: a sharp burst in the direction you're holding, or straight back with nothing held, taken in the same braced skating crouch as boosting, then easing off smoothly rather than stopping dead. Each one costs energy.
  • Space (controller A) jumps: it dips into a quick crouch, pushes off, tucks its legs up and swings them out beneath it. Holding it flies upward on the thrusters. In the air it leans well into the way it's flying, its legs hang nearly straight, swept back behind it the faster it goes, and its back and calf thrusters tip down to hold it up. With boost on in the air it glides instead of dropping.
  • Thrusters run on the energy gauge, a thin bar right across the bottom of the lock box round the crosshair. Empty it and it redlines: the bar flashes red and you can't boost or fly until it recovers. Energy comes back quickly once you stop drawing on it, and faster on the ground.
  • It leans into the way it's going, and its thrusters fire from whichever side pushes it there: the back ones going forward, the side ones strafing, the chest ones going backwards. Its legs fold up smoothly as it leaves the ground and its feet stay planted flat, up and down steps too. Skating over a pit, its feet ease over the gap rather than dropping to the bottom. Its thruster flames taper to a point.
  • It steps up one-block ledges on foot and two-block ledges while boosting. Run into a wall or a building while boosting and it smashes a hole and carries on; at full boost it ploughs straight through with barely a stumble. Walking into one just stops it. Anything small in its way gets trampled, and a hard landing knocks goblins over.
  • It's armed, with a weapon on each trigger. Each has its own load and reloads by itself once it's empty. Above the speedometer, in the bottom corner, each weapon's button, what it has loaded and its name are laid out as they're carried (shoulders above, arms below), in a panel that keeps the same size whatever it says, and up either side of the lock box round the crosshair a tall gauge for each weapon shows how full it is, or how far along its reload is. The gauges and the energy bar are see-through, so they don't hide what you're aiming at: - Right click (controller RB): the Rivet Rifle in its right hand. Hold it for a steady stream of rounds. Its rounds chip at walls: about half the time a round breaks the block it hits. - Left click (controller LB): the Heat Cleaver on its left arm. It lunges forward (at a locked target if one's close, ending with it in reach), easing smoothly off rather than halting, then swings. The lunge adds to however you're already moving, so walking or boosting you keep going through the attack. It reaches across to its right side, its whole upper body turning with it and its right arm lifting out of the way, the blade ignites from its left hand, a flat blade held level, and it whips the blade from right to left in a wide, level arc round in front of it that cuts down goblins, tears into vehicles and slices clean through walls. The swing follows your aim: locked on to something low, or looking down, it bends forward at the hips and slices downward, so it can reach goblins below it and cut into the ground; aimed high it leans back and slices upward. The blade retracts into its hand as it settles back. It needs a moment to cool between swings. - Q (controller LT): the Swarm Pod on its left shoulder. Hold it and the pod locks on to whatever's in the lock box, up to four locks, then let go and a homing missile flies at each. With nothing locked, a tap fires one where you're aiming. - E (controller RT): the Breaker Cannon on its right shoulder. On the ground it stops, braces and swings the cannon over its shoulder before firing a heavy shell that blasts a crater. In the air it fires at once and the kick shoves it backwards. It sits up on a post, so firing low it clears the Gear's own chest.
  • Lock-on: anything in the box round the crosshair is picked out by itself, and the rifle and blade aim at it, leading a moving target. Middle click (controller R3) holds on to it with a hard lock, and the camera turns to keep it in view while you move round it. Press it again to let go.
  • It has 600 health. Wreck it and you're thrown out.
  • X (controller L3) is the assault boost. It crouches and glows for a moment with a rising whine, then dashes off fast straight where you're aiming, up or down too, turning only slowly after your aim. Its weapons still fire on the way. It costs a chunk of energy to start and keeps drawing on it, and it smashes straight through walls. Press it again, or pull back, to stop.
  • Press it again near the target you're locked on to and it flies at it with a kick instead: not much damage, but it'll nearly always stagger what it hits.
  • Staggering: every hit fills a target's impact meter, which drains again once the hits stop. A full meter staggers it for 2 seconds: it can't move or shoot, and takes half again as much damage. Heavy blows (the kick, the Breaker Cannon, the Heat Cleaver) fill it faster. Tanks, helicopters and the Mobile Gear itself can all be staggered.
  • Your own impact meter is the thinner bar under the energy bar, with STAGGERED flashing under it while you are. A locked tank or helicopter shows its health and its impact meter under its bracket.
  • R (controller D-pad down) uses a repair kit, putting back 35% of its health over a second. Each Mobile Gear carries three, and the controls list on the left says how many are left.
  • Its controls can be rebound in Options, in their own "Piloting a Mobile Gear" section.
  • Mobile Gears aren't kept when you leave the Testing Environment.
  • While you're piloting, the controls line for on foot is hidden.

PvP

  • A new PvP switch in Options, off unless you turn it on. With it on, players can fight each other, in split screen or online, whoever they're playing as.
  • Lock on to another player as you would a goblin, on foot, from a tank or helicopter, or with a Mobile Gear's lock box, and its missiles home in on them. A player in a vehicle is locked on as the vehicle.
  • Every attack lands on other players: bites and uppercuts, stomps, ground pounds, ground spikes, trampling, rolls, dashes, blast dashes and blast long jumps, and Gnash's club, dagger, guns and grenades. A hippo's blows hit another player for about a third of what they do to a goblin, so a fight lasts a while.
  • Vehicles too: run another player down in a car or a tank, shoot them with a tank's or helicopter's guns, shells and rockets, or with a Mobile Gear's rifle, blade, missiles and cannon (it tramples them as well).
  • A player killed by another is told who did it, the winner scores 500 points, and the obituaries name the killer. You get up again as usual.
  • Online, the host's switch decides for everyone. A guest's PvP switch shows the host's choice and can't be changed.

Vehicle controls

  • In every vehicle, its controls are listed down the left side of the screen, one to a line, instead of along the bottom. The controls line for on foot is hidden while you drive.

Debug menu

  • Vehicles: pick any vehicle from the list and press Spawn, and one is set down ahead of you, parked and empty, ready to get into. It works in the ordinary game too, not just the Testing Environment: anywhere with a level patch of ground in front of you, and what you spawn stays where you left it.
  • Infinite ammo: while it's on, nothing that has to be reloaded ever runs out (the Mobile Gear's blade still has to cool between swings).

Bullets break blocks

  • Rounds from guns, the Mobile Gear's rifle, Gnash's rifle and handgun, the helicopter's machine gun and the militia's mounted guns, now break the block they hit about half the time, so sustained fire slowly chews through cover.

Less prop pop-in

  • Props out in the open (patio tables, beach and harbour gear, the stadium's fittings and the like) are now drawn out to 64 blocks, up from 34, and furniture indoors stays at 34 as before. The distance is now the same in every direction, so things on the diagonal no longer show up later than things straight ahead.
  • Props now grow up out of the ground over a moment as they come into range, instead of suddenly appearing.
  • New PROPS slider in Options, under Render distance: set how far props are drawn, from 32 to 192 blocks. Furniture indoors goes about half as far as the setting.

Weather

  • Foggy weather now really closes in. The weather's fog used to shrink with the FAR VIEW setting, so with the view turned up it all but vanished; now fog, rain and snow limit how far you can see by the same number of blocks whatever the setting, with thick fog and blizzards down to about 30 blocks.
  • Windy weather is gone.
  • Tanks, helicopters, planes and Mobile Gears now gather snow on top as cars do, as deep as the snow on the ground while they stand out in the open. It blows off a plane in the air or a helicopter going fast.
  • The main menu's "New city" button is now "New world".

Earlier updates

Version 0.4

Play 0.4

Goblins climb vehicles too

  • Gnash and Grizzle can now spider-climb vehicles just as Gus and Gertie do: hold climb and walk or jump into a car, a lorry, a train, a boat's hull, a container stack or a plane to grab its side and hang on while it goes, climb up on to the roof, or walk off the roof's edge with climb held to drop on to its side. Holding climb as a train comes by catches hold of it instead of being run down. Your view stays outside the vehicle while you cling to it.

Grenades break things

  • Grenades now blow holes in the world: one going off takes out a shallow crater in the ground and the walls, fences and anything else built within about three blocks of it, the way tank shells and helicopter rockets already did. A building that loses enough of itself comes down. That goes for Gnash's grenades and for the ones preppers and militia lob at you, so cover doesn't last forever.

Flying planes with WASD

  • On the keyboard, planes now fly with WASD like a flight stick: W pushes the nose down and S pulls it up, A and D bank and turn as before, and Space and C open and close the throttle (C with the throttle shut brakes on the runway). On the runway, get up to speed and hold S to take off. Controllers fly just as before, and the prompt when you climb in shows whichever you're using.

Tidier display

  • The goblin count under your score now reads "GOBLINS SLAIN" instead of "FLATTENED".
  • The card beside the clock now shows just the day, the time of day and the season, with no symbols. The weather, the moon and where you are have moved to a new info pane under the clock, which you switch on from the debug menu (Overlays, Info pane). It's off to begin with.
  • "Sunny" weather is now called "Clear".
  • Full screen is a big button now, in the title screen's menu under New city and Options, and in the pause menu's column of buttons above Quit to title.
  • The pause menu's This rampage card now counts goblins slain, cars totaled, buildings demolished (each building you bring down counts once) and settlements razed (every building in a town or village brought down). Its wrecked bar is gone; how much of the place you're in is down still shows on the card in the bottom right of the screen, which now says Settlement razed.
  • Score numbers, Damage numbers, Shouts and Health bars under On screen in Options now start off, and so does the Debug menu switch. Anything you've already switched on stays on.

Police handguns for Gnash

  • Kill a police officer while playing as a goblin and they drop their handgun. Walk over it to pick it up. It shoots where you look like the rifle, a little weaker and out to 30 blocks rather than 48, but it fires again much sooner. The weapon button now goes club, dagger, handgun, rifle.

A new look

  • The game has a new coat of paint: flat crayon colours, thick ink outlines with hand-drawn corners, and a chunky pixel font (Jersey 10) for titles, buttons and numbers. Anything you read, like descriptions and these notes, keeps the easy-to-read font.
  • Button prompts are drawn as the buttons themselves in the new style: crayon-coloured face buttons and paper keycaps.
  • A real title screen: the logo top left, and Gus himself stomping along a street in front of a scrolling city while you choose, flattening a goblin with every stomp. It's lit and shaded just like the game. The menu card on the right has Play, Co-op, Play online, New city, Options and Full screen, with the Testing Environment and Dev in a small Workshop line underneath, and a sticker for what's new.
  • The enemy hostility slider has moved from the main screen to the top of Options.
  • In Options, each slider's name now sits to its left on the same row, with its setting on the right and the description underneath, and all the sliders line up. In a narrow window the name goes back above the slider.
  • Picking a character is now "Who's stompin'?": four big cards for Gus, Gertie, Gnash and Grizzle, with a 1P tag on player one's pick and a 2P tag on player two's in co-op. In split-screen co-op each of you picks your own character from your own pause menu's Character screen, and a strip under the cards lists what that character can do with your current ability switches. Picking the other player's character still swaps you over.
  • In split-screen co-op each player still has their own pause menu, driven only by their own controller. Player two's is just Resume, Map, Obituary, Character and Controls: what changes the game for both of you (Options, New city, Play online, the Testing Environment, Quit to title) stays in player one's.
  • The title screen's menu card no longer shows a scroll bar down its side when the game first opens.
  • Each player has their own ability switches. In split-screen co-op player two sets theirs in their own pause menu's Character screen, and they go with you whichever hippo you pick, so Gus and Gertie are still the same as each other: it's the player, not the hippo, who has the moves. Player one keeps the switches they had, and player two starts with a copy of them.
  • The ability switches are laid out as basic moves and their upgrades: each basic move (Bite, Stomp, Ground Pound, Dash, Sprint, Jump, Long Jump, Air Dash, Spider Climb) on the left, with the upgrades that improve it trailing off it as yellow tokens, each with a +1 or +2 coin for how many steps up it is (Bite combo, Charge Stomp, Full Charge Stomp, Ground Spike, Rocket Boost and so on). Crash Landing and Fall damage immunity are upgrades to every landing. No more tick boxes: a move that's on is in full colour and one that's off is greyed out, with On or Off in its corner. Click it, or press A on a controller, to switch it. An upgrade whose basic move is off is greyed with a dashed edge and says what it needs; switching it on switches that on too.
  • On a controller, A on a character card picks them and takes you back ("Ready!"), and Y jumps down to the ability switches. Screens opened from a menu have a Back button: B or Esc goes back, and LB / RB (Q / E on the keyboard) jump between screens.
  • The pause menu is a column of big buttons (Resume, Map, Obituary with its count, Character, Controls, Options, Quit to title) beside a "This rampage" card: goblins slain, your score, your best combo, cars totaled, buildings demolished and settlements razed, with the name of the place you're in and the time of day. Underneath are this game's own controls: switch between solo and co-op, Play online, New city and the Testing Environment.
  • Map, Obituary, Character, Controls, Options and Patch notes open in the same card, sliding in from the right, and always start you back on the menu's own buttons when you pause again. B or Esc on the menu itself resumes.
  • Quit to title takes you back to the title screen with Gus stomping along; your city, score and everyone in it are kept, so Play carries on where you left off.
  • Patch notes and Dev are small tags in the card's corner now. In split-screen co-op the card's corner says 1P MENU (green) or 2P MENU (pink), so you can tell whose menu it is, and player two's menu still leaves out Options, Dev, the Testing Environment, Quit to title and the city controls.
  • Options, Controls, the Map, the Obituary and the Patch notes are on paper in the new style. The Dev tools and the Testing Environment keep a dark panel, in the new colours.
  • The HUD is redrawn to match: your score big in the top left with the goblins you've slain under it (and a sticker when the police are after you), pixel hearts and the stamina bar in one paper panel at the bottom, the day and season on a paper card beside a new clock disc, a round minimap, and the speedometer in ink and yolk.
  • Charging a stomp shows STOMP, then BIG STOMP, then shouts FULL CHARGE! when it's all the way up.
  • Combos: every goblin flattened or thing smashed within 3 seconds of the last adds one to your combo, shown under your score with a bar that drains until the next. The combo multiplies your points: +10% for each step from the second, so x11 doubles them and x21 or more triples them (that's as high as it goes). Big combos get a name and a shout: x5 Stompin'!, x10 STOMPTACULAR!, x15 Hippo-critical!, x20 GOBLINGEDDON!, x30 Unstoppable lump!, x50 HIPPOCALYPSE!, x75 Wallow of doom! and x100 Legend of the bog!
  • Points float up from wherever you earned them, damage numbers pop off the goblins as you hit them, and a goblin going down gets a shout: SPLAT! for a stomp, CHOMP! for a bite, KABOOM! for an explosion, and so on. Options, under On screen, has a switch each for Damage numbers, Shouts (the goblin-flattening shouts, combo milestones and FULL CHARGE!, which turns into a plain FULL) and Health bars (bars over the goblins, vehicles and other players near you, off to start with). If you'd switched off the old damage numbers and shouts switch, all three start off.
  • What you've just wrecked is listed in stickers in the top right, newest on top, with the points it earned (combo included), fading as they get older.
  • A Settlement razed card in the bottom right shows how much of the place you're in you've knocked down.
  • In split screen and online, each player has their own combo, pop-ups and list of what they've wrecked, from their own hits. The best combo shows in the pause menu's rampage card, and a new city starts it all again.
  • A Reduced motion switch in Options, under On screen, stops the wobbling, bobbing and popping of the menus and the display and holds Gus still on the title screen. It's on by itself if your system asks for less motion.
  • Sounds for the points as they pop up (rising as your combo climbs) and a fanfare for each combo milestone.
  • Arriving somewhere puts its name up on a big sticker. Chat, voice chips, the controls hint, the debug menu and each co-op player's own pause panel are restyled too, and in split screen each half's name is a 1P-green or 2P-pink tag.

Menus

  • In player two's menu, the button that carries on playing says Resume rather than Co-op.
  • The pause menu's buttons always fit in the window, at any size and in split screen, and never sit in a box with scroll bars.
  • The Map zooms out as far as it takes to see everywhere you've explored at once, however far you've roamed.
  • The highlight round the picked character cards, and their 1P and 2P tags, are no longer cut off at the edges.
  • The What's new sticker sits under the title menu instead of over the Full screen link, and the controller and city seed notes have moved down on to the ground.
  • The pixel-art portraits and hearts are solid at any zoom and on any screen, with no faint lines between their pixels.
  • Options, under On screen, has a new Score numbers switch for the points that float up when you flatten or smash something. Your score and the wreck list still count them either way.
  • The debug menu has a Combo system switch, under Player, and combos are now off to start with. Switched off, there are no combos at all: your points aren't multiplied, and the combo card, combo milestone shouts and the pause menu's Best combo are all hidden. Online, the host's switch counts for everyone.
  • The speedometer only shows while you're in a vehicle, driving or in the gunner's seat.
  • Every ability switch now starts on: Ground Spike, Triple jump, Rocket Boost, Roll, Blast Long Jump and Blast Air Dash join the rest. Blast Dash stays off, since it and Roll are one or the other. Switches you'd already set are kept, but those six are switched on once for you.
  • The "cars hunting you" sticker under your score is gone. The Police pursuit sticker stays.
  • The debug menu no longer has its own Health bars and Damage numbers switches: use the ones in Options, under On screen. Options' Damage numbers now also shows the damage vehicles, trains, boats, rides, tanks, animals and players take, which only the debug menu used to show.
  • Shorter, plainer descriptions on the Options switches.

Goblins

  • A new Goblin pathfinding setting in Options, just under Enemy hostility: Advanced (as before: goblins climb walls and buildings, jump gaps and find their way to you wherever you are) or Simple, the older chase, where goblins run straight at you, go round walls and take a building's stairs, but never climb or jump, so up on a roof you're out of their reach. Switching takes effect at once, and any goblin halfway up a wall lets go and drops down unhurt.

Online co-op

  • Voice chat has two switches of its own, shown under it once it's on: My voice (the others hear you) and Others' voice (you hear them), each with its own volume beside it on the same row, and both volumes go up to 200%. Push to talk sits above them. They replace the Listen only switch: untick My voice to just listen. Push to talk only shows while voice chat is on.
  • Showing chat now always shows everyone's messages: the switch to hide the other players' messages is gone.
  • If you join someone else's game, goblins, cars, other players and everything else moving in the world now glide smoothly instead of jerking from spot to spot when the connection is a bit bumpy. Your game holds a little more in reserve when packets arrive unevenly, and briefly carries things on in the direction they were going if an update is late. Nothing extra is sent over the network, so it's no heavier on the connection.
  • If you join someone else's game, you now see your own score numbers, damage numbers and shouts (they never showed up for anyone but the host), and your combo bar drains as it should instead of sitting full.

The city

  • Cinema marquees now say what's on: two lit boards across the middle of the canopy, each showing a different goblin take on a famous film, like Goblin Wars, Jurassic Goblin, The Silence of the Goblins or Goblins of the Caribbean. Each cinema keeps its own bill, and the boards fall off when you knock the canopy down.

Version 0.3

Play 0.3

Highlights

  • Play online: up to four players in one city over the internet. One hosts, and the others pick the game from the Open games list, or type in its room code for a private game. Find it under Play online on the main menu.
  • Text chat and opt-in voice chat in online games.
  • Two players can share one armed vehicle, one driving and one on the gun.
  • Police draw handguns at range, and every goblin attack has a proper wind-up and strike.

Split-screen co-op menus

  • Both players get the full pause menu now. Press Start to pause your own half of the screen, then Y (or click Pause menu) to open the menu as yours. Its corner says whose menu it is, and only that player's controller moves around it.
  • Player two's menu leaves out what changes the game for both of you: Game options, the Dev tab, the Testing Environment, and the new city, solo, online and hostility controls. Those stay with player one, and so does the debug menu on Select.
  • Picking characters has moved to the Character tab, and the Game tab just says who you're playing as, with a link there.
  • Each player can now be any of the four: Gus, Gertie, Gnash or Grizzle. Pick the one the other player has and you swap over, so you can always tell each other apart. Grizzle wears red, Gnash blue.

Online co-op

  • The host's city is the one you play in. Joining builds it in your browser without touching your own city, and leaving takes you back to yours.
  • Everything the host's game does shows up for the guest: goblins, traffic, trains, planes, boats, rides, smashed blocks, toppling buildings, fires, dust and sound.
  • Your own hippo moves the moment you press, even on a slow connection, and is nudged back into step with the host's game if the two drift apart.
  • Bites and stomps land on goblins where you saw them, not where they've moved to since.
  • Play Gnash online and you can drive, with the chase camera following you.
  • The host can switch to another tab or window and the game carries on for everyone else, so you can also try it out in two tabs of one browser (tick "Two tabs of this browser" in both).
  • Drop out and you're put straight back in your place when the connection comes back (within half a minute).
  • Each player keeps the abilities switched on in their own browser. The host's hostility setting is the game's, and the world never pauses while you're both in it.
  • If you both pick the same hippo, the guest plays the other one, so you can tell each other apart.

Chat and voice chat online

  • Talk to the others in an online game: press Enter, type a message and press Enter again to send it (Esc cancels). Messages show at the bottom left with the sender's name in their colour and fade away after a few seconds. Press Enter again to read back through the recent ones.
  • While you're typing, your keys stay in the chat box: Gus or Gertie stands still instead of walking off.
  • On a controller, D-pad ↑ brings up a few quick messages ("Over here!", "Help!", "Follow me!" and more): pick one with the D-pad and press A to send it, or B to put the list away.
  • Messages are up to 120 characters, and only ever shown as plain text. Typing very fast gets a "slow down" instead.
  • Voice chat, for games over the internet: switch it on in Options, under Online chat & voice. It's off until you switch it on, and it's off again every time you start the game. Nothing touches your microphone until then, and your browser asks first.
  • Push to talk is the default: hold T (D-pad ← on a controller) to talk. Untick it for an open microphone. Both buttons can be changed in the Controls tab.
  • Little chips at the bottom left show who's on voice and light up while they're talking. You can mute any player just for yourself, and set how loud voices are.
  • You only hear players who have voice chat switched on, and with yours off nobody sends you any sound at all.
  • No microphone? You can still listen. Tick "Listen only" and voice chat never asks for a microphone: you hear everyone who's talking, with their chips lighting up, mute and volume as usual. If your microphone is missing or you don't allow it, voice chat switches to listening only by itself and says "No microphone: listening only".
  • Don't want chat? Untick "Show chat" in Options to hide it altogether, or "Other players' messages" to mute what the others type.
  • Enter can't be picked for other controls any more, as it opens the chat.

Open games

  • No more reading out room codes: Play online now lists the games being hosted right now under Open games, with who's hosting and how many players are in. Click Join on one to jump in.
  • Host a game and it shows up on everyone else's list straight away. It drops off when you stop hosting, and shows as Full once all four seats are taken.
  • Want to play with just your friends? Tick "Private game" before or while hosting and your game stays off the list. They can still join with the room code, which works just as before.
  • Works with "Two tabs of this browser" too, for trying it out on one computer.

Goblin attacks

  • Every goblin attack now has a proper wind-up and a strike that suits the weapon. Nothing hits any sooner or later than before: you just get a better look at it coming.
  • Goblins and police lift their club up behind their heads, pause at the top, then chop it down, turning into the blow.
  • Thieves and cultists draw their dagger back to the hip, point first, then thrust it straight in at arm's length.
  • Preppers, militiamen and soldiers swing their rifle round so the stock faces forward, then shove it into you with both hands.
  • Rifle shots: the rifle is brought up to the shoulder, held on you, then kicks when it fires.
  • Grenades are pulled back behind the head and thrown overarm, and cultists raise their fireball high as it grows, then hurl it.
  • Gnash and Grizzle swing the same way: a club drawn back and chopped down, a dagger drawn back and thrust, in your own view and as other players see you. Their arm follows through after a grenade throw.
  • The Dev tab's Enemies viewer marks each attack's wind-up, strike and follow-through on the timeline.

Police handguns

  • Police now carry a handgun in a holster on their belt. Once they've spotted you, they draw it and shoot from 4 to 30 blocks away, then holster it again. Get in close and they swap back to the club.
  • They hold it out in both hands and take aim for a moment first, so you can see the shot coming. When it fires, it kicks their wrists up.
  • Police who come out fighting after you carjack their car carry one too, and so do the Testing Environment's police officers.
  • With enemy hostility turned down, police keep their guns holstered.
  • The Dev tab's Enemies viewer has a new "Draw and fire the handgun" animation for police officers.
  • Online, guests see the gun drawn and the holster, and now see every bullet's tracer, the rifles' too.

Driver and gunner

  • Two goblins can now share one armed vehicle. While one player drives, the other walks up and presses the get-in button to take the gunner's seat. Both local and online co-op work.
  • Technicals and four-wheelers: the gunner works the machine gun or the rifle, aiming wherever they look while the driver steers. You see them standing at the gun in the truck bed, or sitting behind the rider.
  • Tanks and helicopters: the gunner aims the turret, or the helicopter's gun and rockets, with their own view while the driver steers or flies.

Goblins climbing after you

Version 0.1

Play 0.1

Everything from the move to Claude Code on November 2, 2026, up to the first release on GitHub on November 5. Each entry describes where things ended up, so features that were reworked along the way appear once.

Old saves reset. World generation changed many times, so cities saved before this update start fresh.

Highlights

  • Gus can spider-climb and ride moving vehicles, including trains, boats, planes and the roller coaster.
  • Trains, planes, boats, theme park rides and zoo animals can all be destroyed.
  • Cultists, thieves, preppers and militia now defend their sites, and military bases scramble real tanks and helicopters.
  • Tanks and helicopters are proper fighting machines, with machine guns, rockets, shells that reload, lock-on and an aim marker that shows where each shot will really land.
  • Goblins chase with real routes: they climb, jump gaps, swim and use stairs and doors.
  • Almost every building has been resized and furnished room by room, with stairs and bathrooms where they make sense, and outdoor sites are dressed with props.
  • Goblin mode lets you drive anything, and the new Testing Environment spawns any structure, vehicle or enemy on demand.
  • The game runs much faster, and can be played on the web.

Enemies and goblins

Cultists

  • Robed, hooded cultists now gather live at their altars and shuffle around them until disturbed.
  • Come within 15 blocks of the altar or hurt one and the whole cult turns on you.
  • They conjure fireballs at range and stab with ritual daggers up close.
  • Killed cultists stay dead until the cult has had two quiet minutes.

Compound guards

  • Thieves' hideouts, prepper bunkers and militia camps now have live guards who mill about until you come close or hurt one.
  • Thieves stab with daggers.
  • Preppers carry rifles and pipe bombs. Militia carry rifles and grenades.
  • Tower guards hold their posts.

Goblin pathfinding

  • Goblins now find real routes to Gus instead of falling apart in big buildings.
  • They hop ledges and railings, jump gaps, climb walls in both directions, swim, and use stairs and doors.
  • With a clear path, they run straight at where Gus is now rather than where he recently was.
  • They leap at Gus when he's on top of a tall vehicle, and still find him when he's sunk.
  • They walk around furniture, and shove it over if it holds them up.
  • They no longer perch on stair rails, and they swim lower in the water.

Combat, vehicles and destruction

Military bases

  • Parked tanks and helicopters are now real vehicles. Entering a base or hitting one sounds the alert.
  • Tanks hunt you and fire shells. Helicopters lift off, circle, and strafe with bursts and rockets.
  • They're solid. Moving tanks run goblins down and ram cars.
  • Destroyed armour stays destroyed in your save. The base stands down 30 seconds after you leave.

Driving and vehicle combat

  • In goblin mode, press F (Y on a controller) to get in or out of cars, trucks, trains, boats, tanks, helicopters and planes.
  • In a tank, bite fires a shell. In a helicopter, bite (left click or RB) fires a machine gun and stomp (right click or RT) fires rockets. In anything else, bite sounds the horn.
  • Tank shells, the helicopter gun and rockets have magazines and reload times, and an ammo panel shows what's left, with a reload bar and countdown.
  • A driving display shows the vehicle's health, its weapon and a crosshair.
  • Lock on (Q, or R3 on a controller) to tanks and helicopters from your own, and the gun aims ahead of a moving target.
  • The camera sits higher and further back in tanks and helicopters. The aim marker follows the real path of the shell or rocket and marks where it will go off: faint when it would never land on anything, and orange at the screen edge when the spot is off-screen.
  • Tank shells and helicopter rockets blow bigger craters and damage vehicles, which jolt, smoke, burn and show health bars.
  • Crashing aircraft blow craters, and stopped helicopters and wrecks drop when the ground under them goes.
  • Driven cars climb one-block steps.
  • Motorcycles join the traffic and can be ridden in goblin mode.

Trains

  • Trains now have an engine at each end and reverse smoothly at the end of the line.
  • Each car has its own health. Losing one stops the train and scores 500, and destroying them all scores 1000 more. Subway trains can be wrecked too.
  • A moving train throws you forward and up instead of nudging you aside.

Airplanes

  • Land on any airport plane and ride it through taxiing, takeoff, flight and landing.
  • Planes have 300 health and score 1000 points. Shot down, one falls and blows a crater.
  • A replacement arrives two minutes later, once no one's within 350 blocks.
  • Moving planes strike goblins, cars and you. Parked planes no longer clip the terminals.

Boats

  • Every boat is a new model: pedal boat, rowboat, speedboat, launch, yacht, trawler, tug, ferry, warship and container ship.
  • All of them can be stood on, ridden and wrecked. They burn and list when hurt, sink when wrecked, and are replaced later.
  • At sea they follow routes on open water. Others are moored at harbours, quays, dry docks, beaches and rigs.
  • Jump while swimming beside a boat to climb aboard.

Theme parks and zoos

  • Coaster cars, the Ferris wheel and the carousel can all be destroyed. Break the track and the coaster derails.
  • Zoo animals can be killed. Wrecks and deaths are saved with your world.

Traffic and crashes

  • Ordinary traffic now runs goblins down, not just police cars.
  • Trains, boats and planes crash when they meet.
  • A car hitting a wall faster than 13 blocks a second crushes a hole sized by its speed, takes heavy damage and carries on through. Slower bumps still bounce off.

News crews

  • News crews now drive out from news stations to cover fires, car crashes and killings.

Movement

  • Riding vehicles: Drop on to any moving vehicle and you land on it unhurt, unless you fell from a real height. You're carried round bends and stay on the roof going down ramps.
  • Roller coaster: Gus can now land on and ride coaster cars for whole laps.
  • Climbing vehicles: Gus can cling to and climb cars, trucks, train cars, ship hulls, container stacks and planes while they move. Climb over the top to ride on the roof, or walk off the roof with climb held to tip on to the side. Hold climb as a vehicle hits you to grab on.
  • Spider climb: Gus can now climb on to pillars and one-block-deep ledges. He grabs walls at a slant, and holding climb stops a sprint from smashing what he's about to climb.
  • Stairs and ramps: Gus can walk up stairs. He stays on ramps going down instead of bouncing, and sprinting uphill no longer breaks the ramp's support.

Buildings and interiors

Bigger, more recognisable buildings

  • Cities and large towns are about 30% wider.
  • Each kind of site has its place: offices downtown, hospitals in the ring, schools and superstores in the suburbs, heavy industry at the edge.
  • Shops, restaurants, banks, libraries and discos get their own roof shapes, with bigger signs.
  • Landmarks are easier to pick out, like the museum's copper dome, the whale on the aquarium and the train station clock tower.

Site sizes, in plots

  • Large: Quarries are 6 x 6. Zoos are 5 x 5. Hospitals, universities, prisons, factories, studios and power stations run from 3 x 3 to 6 x 6.
  • Medium: Bases and dumps are 4 x 4. Lumber camps are 4 x 3. Mines and schools are 3 x 3. Museums, aquariums, furniture stores and cathedrals run from 2 x 3 to 4 x 3. Police stations are 2 x 2 with a rooftop helipad.

Rebuilt and new

  • Fire stations: Red bay fronts, a taller hose tower and a flag.
  • Schools: A new larger site with a bus lane, clock tower and playing field.

Furnished rooms

  • Restaurants, shops, stores, malls, homes, flats, offices, public buildings, hospitals, venues and factories have laid-out rooms.
  • Museums have themed galleries, including Egypt, knights and fossils. Sound stages each shoot a different set.
  • Street benches, litter bins and beach and dock furniture are now models too.

Stairs and bathrooms

  • Every multi-storey building that can fit stairs now has them, drawn as real steps with handrails. They can be smashed.
  • Buildings that were too cramped have been enlarged so stairs, bathrooms and doors all fit.
  • Bathrooms now appear wherever they'd logically be, sized to the floor, with matching walls and swinging doors.
  • The university library has a second staircase at its far end.

Outdoor sites

  • Farmer's markets, camps, campsites, the cult altar, building sites, nurseries, trailer parks, cemeteries, mines, quarries, lumber camps, prepper, militia and thieves' compounds, ruins, dumps, parks, plazas, courts, lighthouses, power stations, zoos, theme parks, stadiums, arenas, car parks, bunkers and wrecked cars are all dressed with props.
  • Campsites have motorhomes parked on their pitches, and they can be wrecked like any parked car.
  • Campfires, the cult altar's included, are real fires now, but they don't spread and only hurt someone standing right on the wood.

World, roads and coast

Roads and traffic

  • Highways no longer slice through big buildings, and they join up properly with interchanges in any built-up place.
  • Barriers no longer cut across lanes where roads meet, and highways no longer stop dead in the grass.
  • Ramps taper to street width and carry their road markings all the way down.
  • Streets have solid centre lines that meet cleanly at corners, plus white stop lines. Trees stay off the streets.
  • Traffic flows better, with longer green lights, proper following and lanes that merge before end ramps. The worst queues dropped from 12 to 15 cars down to 7 to 10.
  • Fixed a jam where long trucks froze at the foot of ramps.

Coast

  • Beaches and cliffs now blend gradually, and the sea bed is smooth.
  • Places by the water become coastal cities, harbor towns, resort towns or fishing villages. Shipping ports belong to coastal cities.
  • The kind of place now shows under its name.

Parody brands

  • About 40 more parody chains, nine new billboards, and about 90 chains with their own goblin take on a logo.

Looks

  • Distant buildings now match their full-detail look, with their real wall, window and roof colours, proper shading, lit windows at night, and grass, rain and snow that follow the seasons and weather.
