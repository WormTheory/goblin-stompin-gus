Version 0.5

Shape your own world

  • New world now lets you shape the world before it's made, with the World Generator's sliders: Land & sea, Land shape, Rivers and Climate. Left alone, they make the world just as before.
  • The settings are saved with the world, so it's made the same way every time you load it, and online friends get the host's world.
  • New world and the World Generator have a Settlements group too: Towns (how many places there are), City size (how big cities and large towns grow), Clustering (from evenly spaced to gathered in clumps with open country between) and Country sites (how many farms, bases, airfields, mines, campsites and the like). The seed line says "custom" when a world has settings of its own.
  • New world has a Seed box. Leave it empty for a random world, or type a number or any word or phrase: the same seed always makes the same world. The World Generator's seed box takes words too, so a seed tried there makes the same world in the game.
  • The World Generator has a Noise view: just the raw maps a world is made from (land and sea, temperature, moisture, biomes, ruggedness, height, where rivers tend to rise, and where towns are likely), drawn in about a second and sharpening as you watch. The sliders change it as you drag them. Switch back to Map for everything that's built.
  • The Noise view's Rivers map marks where rivers and streams really rise once you zoom in, its Towns map also shows where places come out smaller (in rugged country, and in biomes with smaller towns), and pointing at the Land map tells you how far above or below the sea it is. Height draws about three times faster zoomed far out.
  • The World Generator loads a new seed or new settings without loading the game again, so changes show up sooner, and it fits better on a phone.

Territories

  • The world is divided into territories now, each made up of many settlements: kingdoms, principalities, duchies, republics, free states, nations, empires, khanates, tribal lands and more, each with a name of its own and a capital.
  • Their borders follow the land: coastlines, rivers, lakes and mountain ranges.
  • The pause map draws their borders and names over what you've explored, the minimap shows a border you're near, and a banner names the territory you've come into.
  • New world and the World Generator have a Territories group: Size (how big territories are on average) and Variety (how much they differ). The World Generator shows them as a layer on the Map, and in the Noise view.
  • Fixed the World Generator sometimes showing only sea after an update, when the browser kept an older copy of its map maker.

Biomes, polished

  • Waterfalls drop into a proper plunge pool now, about six blocks deep under the fall and shallower toward the rim, deep enough to dive into.
  • Where a river meets a lake it runs down to the lake's level in a steady slope, with no more steps like little weirs.
  • The land behind a rocky headland rises into the hills inland, instead of lying flat behind it.
  • The pause map marks stone arches, waterfalls and landmarks you've explored, and colours natural shores by kind: sandy shores, shingle, ice shelves, mangroves, red sand, cliffs and headlands, and fjords. Each has an entry in the key. The World Generator's Map has the same, as its Wonders and Coasts layers.
  • Out in the far view, the oil fields, peat diggings, research stations, safari lodges, stilt villages and ski slopes look like themselves (the rigs and tanks, the trenches, the radar dome and mast, the tents and waterhole, the huts over the water, the piste and its lift) rather than plain patches of ground.
  • Biomes blend into each other now: where two meet, patches of each mix across a wide band instead of meeting at a sharp line or in a speckle of single blocks, and the grass and leaves change colour gradually across it, in the far view too.
  • Farms, compounds, bases and other sites out in the country no longer sit in a pit below the hills with cliffs all round them: they're only built where the land round them is low enough to meet them gently.
  • Where one kind of ground meets another (grass and sand, sand and red sand, dirt and snow), their colours fade into each other over some fifty blocks instead of stopping at a block's edge.
  • Towns, farms and roads cut into hills more gently: the terraces beside a town's edge are lower and climb all the way up instead of ending in a cliff, and roads and railways run through wider cuttings with gentler sides.
  • Coasts change gradually as well: a sandy beach gives way to a rocky headland as the land gets rougher, and one kind of shore turns into the next in stretches rather than block by block.
  • Dust devils are easy to see now: tall, swirling funnels of dust that lean with the wind.
  • Towns are built a little more their biome's way: in the desert and the badlands, small shops and eateries are adobe with parapets and log beam ends, squares are more often paved plazas, and there are more rows of shops; in forests, swamps and the cold they're log and plank with false fronts or steep roofs, and the squares are left green; on the moor they're stone.
  • This changes how the world is made, so a saved city starts afresh.

The world

  • The world is made of continents and oceans now, about half land and half sea. Before, one side of the world was all ocean and the other all land. Now there are big continents and smaller ones, seas and oceans between them, island chains off their coasts and the odd group of islands far out at sea.
  • The sea is shallow over the continental shelf near the coast and deep out in the open ocean.
  • Continents have bold, solid coastlines, with big peninsulas, bays and gulfs. Island chains off the coasts are made of whole, rounded islands, with the sea bed rising smoothly round each one.
  • You now always start on a continent, never on an island off one.
  • The World Generator zooms out more than ten times further, far enough to see whole continents and oceans. Far out it shows just the land, the sea and the capital; settlements, bases and rivers come back as you zoom in, and markers that would pile on top of each other are thinned out.
  • The World Generator has Land & sea sliders: how much land there is, how big the continents are, how rough their coastlines, how many ocean channels cut through them and how many island chains lie offshore. The settings go in the page's address, so a world can be shared as you've shaped it. Island size makes the islands in a chain bigger and fewer, with fewer tiny specks between them. The game's own worlds are unchanged.
  • This makes a new world, so a saved city starts afresh.
  • The World Generator can show the climate over the land, as temperature or moisture: the groundwork for biomes. It doesn't change the game's world yet.

Biomes

  • The wilds between towns now have a climate, and with it twelve biomes: grassland, temperate forest, taiga, tundra, ice fields, desert, badlands, scrubland, savanna, rainforest, swamp and moor. Each region of them is thousands of blocks across, and you always start in grassland.
  • Each biome has its own ground (snow, sand over sandstone, red sand over banded clay, mud), its own trees (pines, acacias, jungle giants, drooping swamp trees, cactus and dead trees), its own ponds (frozen in the ice fields, muddy and reedy in the swamp) and its own shade of grass and leaves, in towns too.
  • Biomes are seen from afar, and shown on the map and the minimap.
  • The World Generator can show the biomes, and has Climate sliders: warmer or colder, wetter or drier, how big the climate regions are and how strongly it gets colder one way and hotter the other.
  • The woods round sites in the wilds (campsites, lumber camps, thieves' hideouts, ruins) grow the biome's own trees.
  • River banks are the biome's ground, muddy in the swamp.
  • The debug menu's Info pane shows the biome you're standing in, apart from the place.
  • This makes a new world, so a saved city starts afresh.

Mountains and lakes

  • Out in the wilds the land now rises into hills and mountain ranges, up to about 130 blocks, with bare rock near the top, snow on the peaks (lower the colder it is) and a new Alpine biome up high.
  • Towns, farms, sites, roads, railways and rivers all stay on the level: the mountains rise away from them, so towns sit in valleys and rivers run along valley floors.
  • Roads and railways cut through the hills and tunnel under the high ground (road tunnels are lined and lit).
  • Lakes lie in the low, wet country, frozen over in the ice fields.
  • Mountains are seen from afar, and the map and minimap show the hills shaded, with the rock, snow and lakes.
  • The World Generator has a Height layer and Land shape sliders: more or fewer mountains, how high they get, how hilly the rest is and how many lakes there are.
  • The World Generator shows the ground's height and biome wherever you point, and can lay hill shading and contour lines over any map.
  • The World Generator has a 3D view: the middle of the map, just as it's shown, raised up to the ground's height, to turn round and tilt, with a slider for how much to exaggerate the heights.
  • The World Generator has River sliders: how many rivers there are, how much they meander, how far they wander and how wide they are. The game's own worlds are unchanged.
  • Highways cross the deeper lakes on a deck on pillars, with the water running underneath.
  • Railway tunnels are lined and lit, like the road tunnels.
  • Fields of big boulders lie at the mountains' feet, and loose scree just below the bare rock.
  • Big blasts carve their craters into the hills and mountains as they stand, rather than at street level.
  • Towns in rugged country are fewer and smaller.
  • The map and minimap shade the land as it really is beside the roads, rivers and towns.
  • There are no lakes near the start, as there are no mountains there.
  • Planes flying their rounds climb over the hills and mountains.
  • Beside towns, farms and sites the land climbs in terraces held up by stone retaining walls, rather than sheer rock faces.
  • Sites in the wilds keep away from the most rugged country.
  • This makes a new world again, so a saved city starts afresh.

Landforms

  • Deserts are rolling seas of dunes, long sandy ridges with a steep face on the side away from the wind.
  • The badlands rise into flat-topped mesas and plateaus with stepped sides of banded clay, and canyons cut down through them. Scrubland has lower plateaus.
  • In middling hilly country the land steps up sheer rock cliffs that run on for kilometres.
  • The driest, hottest deserts have dead-flat white salt flats.
  • Small lakes called tarns lie in bowls high in the mountains, frozen over where it's cold.
  • Oases dot the deserts: a pool of water ringed with grass and palm trees, the dunes dying away round it.
  • Sinkholes drop straight down toward the caves, with water at the bottom in warm country.
  • This makes a new world again, so a saved city starts afresh.

Weather by region

  • Roads no longer snow over like a field. Traffic keeps its wheel ruts clear in every lane, on town streets, crossroads, motorways and country highways, with the snow left along the kerbs, down the middle and on the hard shoulder. In the heaviest snow the ruts pack down grey instead of staying bare.
  • The weather now depends on where you are. Rain falls as snow in the cold north and high in the mountains, the ice fields get blizzards, and the desert stays dry.
  • Deserts and badlands get sandstorms: the air turns ochre, dust blows past and you can hardly see.
  • The savanna and scrubland have dusty days, with dust devils whirling across the plain.
  • The rainforest gets heavier rain, and a humid haze on its drier days. Moors and swamps fog over more often.
  • Snow lies by temperature. The tundra and ice fields stay white all year, even on a clear summer day. Cold country keeps its snow far into spring, and it melts fast in warm places.
  • Winter only freezes ponds and rivers where it's cold enough, never in the desert, savanna or rainforest.
  • In split screen, each player sees the weather where they are.

Coasts

  • Coasts now match the land behind them. Where the country is rugged, the shore stands up out of the sea as tall rocky headlands.
  • Cold shores are shingle: gravel, cobbles and the odd bit of driftwood.
  • Frozen shores have an ice shelf you can walk out on, with floes drifting beyond its edge.
  • Warm, wet shores grow mangroves, trees standing on arched roots in shallow, muddy water.
  • Beaches in the badlands are red sand.
  • This makes a new world again, so a saved city starts afresh.

Living in each biome

  • Towns are built for their country. Desert and badlands towns are stucco, sandstone and banded clay with flat roofs. Taiga, tundra and ice-field towns are log and plank cabins with steep roofs for the snow. Moor villages are stone cottages.
  • The harsh country has fewer and smaller places: the ice fields, tundra, desert, badlands and rainforest are mostly villages, and there are no cities there.
  • Townsfolk and goblins dress for the weather: parkas and fur hoods in the cold, head wraps and robes in the desert, reed hats in the swamp, safari hats on the savanna, flat caps and scarves on the moor.
  • Coming into a new biome out in the open puts its name on screen, and a place's banner says which biome it's in.
  • Six new sites out in the country, each only in its own biome: - oil fields in the desert, with a drilling rig, nodding pumpjacks and storage tanks; - peat diggings on the moor; - research stations in the ice fields, with a radar dome and a helipad; - safari lodges on the savanna, with tents, a watchtower and a waterhole; - stilt villages in the swamp, joined by boardwalks; - ski slopes in cold mountains, with a chairlift up the piste.
  • The other country sites suit the land too: lumber camps are in the forests, more mines and ruins are in the desert, and more cult altars are in the swamp. There are no farms in the ice fields.
  • This makes a new world again, so a saved city starts afresh.

Wonders of the wilds

  • Waterfalls pour over the steeper drops in the hills into a pool at the foot.
  • Stone arches stand in the desert, badlands and scrub.
  • Rugged coasts have sea stacks offshore, and now and then a sea arch you can sail under.
  • Cold mountains carry glaciers: ice flowing down below the snow line, with crevasses across it.
  • Cold, rugged coasts are fjords: sheer rock walls up to 40 blocks high, with deep water right under them.
  • Each biome has its own landmark, far apart and well away from roads: a giant's ribs in the dry country, a great tree in the forests, a great baobab on the savanna, a wandering stone on the tundra, and standing stones elsewhere.
  • Where-you-are names a wonder when you're beside it.
  • Mountain tarns now show in the far view, at their own height.
  • This makes a new world again, so a saved city starts afresh.

Rivers

  • Rivers now start somewhere and go somewhere: each one rises in the hills or mountains, or flows out of a lake, and runs downhill until it reaches the sea or joins a bigger river.
  • Smaller, shallower streams branch off the rivers and feed into them.
  • Through the hills and mountains, rivers carve valleys for themselves.
  • The World Generator has two more River sliders: how far rivers run inland, and how many streams branch off them. It draws streams thinner than rivers.
  • Each river has a width of its own, the same all the way down (the Width slider still sets the widest).
  • A river passing a lake now runs into it, with no strip of land left between the two, and rises to the lake's level as it reaches it.
  • Swamp river banks are muddy along whole stretches rather than in patches.
  • The map and minimap no longer draw rivers out across the sea.
  • This makes a new world again, so a saved city starts afresh.

Co-op on two screens

  • The Controls screen has a new Players section listing the keyboard and mouse and every controller plugged in, each lighting up as you use it. Give each one to Player 1 or Player 2, so player two can play on the keyboard and player one on a controller, or each player can have a controller of their own however they're numbered. Auto puts it back as it was.
  • Player 2 screen: Split screen, as before, or Second window, which gives player two a window of their own to put on a second monitor. In Chrome and Edge it opens full screen on the other monitor by itself, once you've allowed the game to see your screens; in other browsers drag it across and press Full screen.
  • With Second window chosen, it opens when you click Co-op, or with Open window. Close it and you're back to split screen.
  • Drawing two windows is harder work for the graphics card than split screen.

Saves

  • Your game now carries on just as you left it: your score, goblins slain and everything in This rampage, the time of day, the day and season, and the weather.
  • Each player's health and stamina are kept too, and as a goblin your weapons, ammo, grenades and landmines. Left dead, you come back at full health. Player two gets theirs back on joining.
  • Left in a car, tank, helicopter, plane, hot-air balloon or Mobile Gear, you're back in it, in the same seat, with the damage it had. Anything that was flying comes back on the ground below. Trains, boats and ships can't be brought back, so you start beside where you were.
  • New city starts all of it afresh.

Playing online

  • As a guest, your own shots show the moment you fire: the tracer and the bang no longer wait on the host, and bullet streaks no longer hang in the air.
  • Guests now see grenades, shells, rockets and the Nuke Launcher's warhead in flight, instead of a warhead stuck where it was fired.
  • As a guest you see your own goblin stealing a vehicle and riding anything open, as everyone else does.

Gunfire

  • The flash of light from a gunshot now comes from the barrel, not from a spot partway along the bullet's streak. That goes for every gun: goblins', Gnash's, vehicles', the Mobile Gear's and the carrier's, and online too.
  • Gnash's bullets now start from the tip of the gun in your hands.

Knock everything over

  • Street lamps, traffic lights, signs, flags, billboards, benches, bins and furniture now go over to every attack, not just smashing into them: a bite, roll or dash, Gnash's blades and clubs, bullets, grenades, shells, rockets, fireballs and the Mobile Gear's blade. A bullet stops at the post it hits.

Nukes

  • A nuke looks the part now: a blinding flash, a fireball that cools from white to red and rolls up into a mushroom cloud on a stem of dust, a ring of dust racing out along the ground, a white dome round the fireball for a moment and a collar round the stem.
  • A nuke fired a long way no longer goes off early in mid-air: it flies on until it hits something.
  • The cloud drifts off with the wind and thins away over a minute or so. It's mottled in lighter and darker shades rather than one flat colour.
  • The fireball swells and rolls over into the mushroom's ring in one smooth movement, rather than sliding from a ball into a ring.
  • The mushroom cloud is see-through at its edges like other smoke, its stem flares out into the cap and turns only slowly, the collar round the stem is mottled, and the ring of dust along the ground keeps billowing out, climbing and drifting rather than sitting still. Dust hangs over the whole blast area too, drifting outward, faster the further out it is. The dust settles on the ground under it, down in the crater too, rather than hanging at one height.
  • Water, smoke and dust behind the mushroom cloud and the white dome no longer show through in front of them.
  • Smoke from fires shows through the nuke's dust, and the dust through the smoke.
  • The smoke rising round the edge of the blast fans out, bends over and drifts away rather than standing in columns.
  • All smoke now blows with the wind: burning buildings, campfires and wrecked vehicles too.
  • The boom and the shaking reach you at the speed of sound, so from far off you see it well before you hear it.
  • At night it lights the whole sky and the ground round it orange.

Screen layout

  • The Mobile Gear's weapon readouts are narrower and sit just left of the speedometer, rather than stacked above it.
  • How much of a settlement you've razed is now a bar curved round the top of the minimap, with the place's name and how much of it is down curved along it (on two lines when the name's long), so the speedometer no longer jumps up to make room for a card. It stays up for 5 seconds after you leave a place, then fades away, unless you walk into another place, which takes over at once.
  • Your controls on foot are now listed down the left of the screen, one to a line, where a vehicle's go, and they stay there rather than fading away. In a vehicle its own controls take their place.
  • Next and previous weapon are one line in the controls list now, "switch weapon", shown as the scroll wheel or the D-pad's left and right.
  • Options has a Hide controls switch, which hides the controls list down the left, on foot and in vehicles.
  • The time of day is gone from the card beside the clock, which now shows just the day and season. It's in the debug menu's Info pane instead, as a clock time (and whether time's paused) beside the time of day.

Profiler

  • The debug menu's Overlays have a Profiler: a graph of each frame's time, split by what took it (players, goblins, traffic, the world being built, destruction, drawing, online play), the busiest parts of each, what's being drawn, memory, how many of everything there is, and the last few slow frames with what was happening at the time (a building coming down, a blast, a car going up). It's off until switched on, and hidden in Show / hide until you show it. Beside the CPU's time it shows GPU / wait: the rest of each frame, mostly the graphics card drawing it, so a slow frame with a low CPU time means the graphics card is what's holding it up.

Drivers

  • Everyone at the wheel is dressed as themselves, in their own colours, hats, glasses and the rest, instead of a plain green head, and whoever you haul out or leave in a wreck is dressed the same. Police drivers are in blue, and your own goblin shows at the wheel of whatever you drive.

Switching weapons

  • The weapon bar above your hearts now shows your weapons as a row of little pictures of each one, the one in hand lit up, with landmines and grenades showing how many you have.
  • R no longer switches weapons (the mouse wheel does), and the weapon bar no longer lists buttons: just your weapons and grenades.
  • Grenades are now a weapon like the others: switch to them and attack to throw one, overarm, with the grenade in your hand until it goes. Stomp no longer throws them. Throw your last and you're back to your next weapon.
  • The rounds left in your gun, and the reload prompt, are now in the bottom right corner in big numbers, turning red when the magazine's empty.
  • As a goblin, the mouse wheel and the D-pad now go both ways through your weapons: wheel down or D-pad right for the next, wheel up or D-pad left for the one before. Both can be changed in the Controls tab.
  • Push to talk no longer has a controller button by default (D-pad left was it): give it one in the Controls tab if you play online with a controller.
  • Your weapon in first person no longer shakes as you turn when you're a long way from where you started. Everything close to you is drawn steadier out there too.

Ruined towns

  • A town you've wiped out stays dead. Traffic no longer drives into it: cars on the roads in turn back or head the other way, and none come off the motorway into it. Nobody turns up there, the shops have no staff, nothing's parked, no one's sent out to it, and anyone left wandering about slips away once you're not looking.

Nuclear meltdown

  • Bring down a reactor at a nuclear power station and the siren sounds. A few seconds later the whole place goes up: a blinding flash, a fireball and a shockwave that runs out across the town, flattening it as it goes. The middle is left a scorched crater ringed with rubble, the streets round it are levelled to the ground with a few stumps of wall standing, and further out buildings lose their top floors and the side that faced the blast, and some come down. Fires burn round the edge under a mushroom cloud.
  • Anything caught in it is hurt by how close it was: near the middle there's nothing left of anyone, and further out you're knocked about. Cars, gas pumps and mines in the middle are simply gone rather than setting each other off.
  • The game keeps running smoothly while it happens, and the crater is saved with the world and seen the same by everyone playing online.
  • The Testing Environment has a Big blast button, with a slider for its size, to set one off ahead of you.
  • The crater's rim of rubble now sits on the ground, so it no longer hangs in the air on uneven ground or where two craters overlap.
  • Across the flattened town grass is burnt to dirt, streets are broken up in patches of rubble, and the buildings it brings down leave heaps of rubble behind.
  • The capitol's lawns, stadium pitches, plazas, courts and railways are scarred too: lawns and pitches burnt to dirt, paving and track beds broken up, the rails and sleepers torn up, and rubble left on what's still standing of a stadium's stands.
  • Snow melts wherever the blast reaches, off the ground, the rooftops and anyone or anything caught in it. If it's still snowing, fresh snow settles on the crater from bare ground, and the melted snow stays melted in a save and online.
  • Seen from far off, a flattened town now looks flattened: the buildings it took are gone or left as low heaps, the ones further out are cut down to what's left of them, the ground is scorched and the trees are gone, rather than the whole town still standing on the horizon.
  • Any building brought down or badly smashed, nuke or no nuke, now looks that way from a distance too, even from the furthest edge of the view.

Nuke Launcher

  • A new weapon for goblins, picked in the Loadout: a launcher with a nuclear warhead on the front. It lobs the warhead slowly in a shallow arc, and wherever it lands the same blast as a meltdown goes off, smaller but still enough to flatten a few streets.
  • Fire it level and it lands far enough off for you to live through it. Fire it at a wall in front of you and you won't.
  • One warhead in the tube and two spares, with a long reload. Once the spares are gone it's empty, unless Infinite ammo is on. The weapon bar shows how many warheads it has left in all, as it does for landmines and grenades.
  • The debug menu has a Nuke strength slider to make its blast smaller or bigger, up to twice the size of a meltdown.
  • A warhead landing on or beside an aircraft carrier now tears into the ship itself, wherever it was fired from, not just when fired from its own deck. Hit near the middle, the carrier is lost and what's left of it sinks; further off it's holed, and can break up and sink as it does from any other smashing.
  • A carrier's deck weapons now fall, wrecked, when the deck under them is blown away, and drop into the sea if there's nothing left below.
  • Firing it into the sea no longer freezes the game for a moment: the warhead goes off as it hits the water rather than sinking to the sea bed.
  • Smashing a ship's blocks no longer stalls the game for a few seconds the first time.

Rocket Launcher

  • The bazooka is gone, replaced by the Rocket Launcher, an anti-tank launcher with a sight. Fired as it is, its rocket flies straight and bursts on whatever it hits.
  • Hold stomp to look down its sight. Keep a vehicle in the middle of it and the launcher growls as it tracks it, then locks on with a steady tone. A rocket fired while locked flies up and homes in on that vehicle, even if you've turned away or it moves.
  • A car locked on with the lock button counts too.
  • It hits vehicles harder than the bazooka did. A Loadout that had the bazooka switched on now has the Rocket Launcher.
  • An enemy firing one at you while you're driving locks on to your vehicle and its rocket chases you.

Swimming

  • You now float at the surface instead of slowly sinking, so there's no need to keep paddling to stay up.
  • Hold climb (C, controller LB) to dive, and let go to bob back up. Near a wall it still grabs on and climbs.
  • A stroke at the surface throws you up far enough to climb out on to a one-block ledge.
  • Gnash and Grizzle can now paddle upward with jump in the water, as Gus and Gertie do.
  • Splashes and the wake while swimming are smaller, sized to whoever's swimming, and trail out behind you rather than in front, so they no longer spray across Gnash's and Grizzle's view.

Mobile Gear on water

  • With its boost on, the Mobile Gear skates across water at any speed, throwing up spray. Switch the boost off and it drops in with a splash.
  • Skating over bushes and one-block bumps no longer shakes it up and down. It now eases over each step.

Mobile Gear assault boost

  • The assault boost no longer gets stuck in buildings and trees. Going through them at an angle it used to catch on the corners of the hole it made and stop dead; now it smashes straight on through. Boosting upward into a ceiling smashes through that too.
  • During an assault boost, hold jump (Space) to climb and boost (C) to dive. Aiming down into a dive works properly now too, rather than sinking slowly.
  • When an assault boost ends, the Mobile Gear keeps its speed and slows down over a second or so, rather than stopping almost at once.
  • Skating flat out into a solid wall or building now smashes through it, as it was meant to, rather than stopping at it.
  • The shoulder cannon no longer swings round to where it last fired when you press its button. It points where you're aiming while it braces.

Fixes

  • The minimap's arrow now shows which way you're facing while you're in a vehicle.
  • The airliners have their windows and coloured stripe back.
  • Online, a guest aiming up or down reaches the host again, so their shots go where they're looking rather than straight out level.

Lighting

  • At night, lights now light up what's around them. Street lamps pool light on the pavement, neon signs throw their own colours across the street, lit windows glow on the walls and rooms beside them, and lamps, TVs and screens light the rooms they're in. Walls block the light, so it stays in the room or the street it's in. Smash a lamp or a sign and its light goes out.
  • It's switched on or off with Block lighting under Sound & picture in Options. Off, the game looks and runs exactly as before.
  • A building that's been destroyed or brought down goes dark: its windows, neon and signs stop glowing, and the pieces of it falling don't glow either.
  • In fog, rain and snow at night, lit windows, neon, lamps and the light they cast now show through the murk as a soft glow, some way past where the buildings themselves disappear.
  • In thick fog the sky and clouds are now lost in the fog too, so far-off towers no longer stand out as dark shapes against a clear sky.
  • The debug menu has a Fog light slider for how strongly lights show through fog.
  • Fires and campfires now light up the ground and walls around them at night, dimming as they die down.

Crashes

  • Vehicles now crash by their weight. A bus ploughs on through a car and shoves it aside, while a car that hits a bus mostly stops dead. Hit a car on its back corner and it spins round; traffic spun out stops steering for a moment, then sets off again.
  • Hit hard enough side-on, a tall vehicle such as a van tips over on its side, and is righted a little later.
  • Something much heavier can throw a lighter vehicle up over its bonnet. Bicycles and scooters hit at speed throw their riders off and cartwheel away.
  • Damage depends on how hard the hit really was, so a gentle nudge does nothing and a big one hurts, more for the lighter of the two.
  • Your own car spins when hit too, but you keep steering.
  • Walls: a glancing blow turns you off along the wall rather than stopping you, and whether you smash through depends on your weight as well as your speed, so a bus goes through where a car bounces off.
  • Gus and Gertie trample cars with their weight: a motorbike spins away, a bus barely moves.
  • Trains, planes, boats and ships crash by their weight too. A ship ramming a boat hardly feels it while the boat takes the lot, and a car that hits a train or a plane is knocked back and spun round by it.
  • A car wrecked at speed that slides into another car knocks it aside instead of stopping dead, and one spinning as it's wrecked keeps spinning as it slides.

New weapons and reloading

PvP

Vehicle controls

Keys only clash within the same set, so the same key can do different things in different vehicles. Steering and the throttle are still the walking keys and the left stick; Get in / out, Lock on and the online chat keys work everywhere.

  • Every vehicle has instruments of its own in the bottom-right corner in place of the one speedometer: a dashboard with a rev counter for cars, a digital display that shows the lean on motorcycles, a truck dial with an air-pressure gauge, a fan and slip gauge on the hovercraft, the mast height, tilt and tip risk on the forklift, the gear range and implement on the tractor, a turret and hull compass on the tank, an artificial horizon with height and airspeed on the helicopter, round dials in the two-seater, a glass cockpit display in the airliner, an engine telegraph and rudder on boats and ships, oars on the rowing boat, a brake and power lever on trains and the wind at each height in the balloon. The fighter jet's display is drawn over the whole view, like a real head-up display.

  • The forklift's forks work. Hold Space to raise them and C to lower them (RB and LB on a controller), and hold Left Shift (X) with either to tilt the mast. Raise them under a crate, a goblin or a light car and it comes up with you. Lower it on to anything, a ledge, a roof or the ground, and it's set down there. Drive at a goblin with the forks up and they spear it. The forklift steers with its back wheels, so it turns almost on the spot with its tail swinging wide, and it beeps when it reverses. Take a sharp turn with a load held high and it tips over on its side, throwing you out; get back in to rock it back on to its wheels.

  • Theme park rides and zoo animals have health bars (Options' Health bars), like every other vehicle: each coaster car, the Ferris wheel and each of its gondolas, the carousel, and every animal.
  • The tractor has a hand throttle: R steps it up through low, middle and high, Left Shift steps it down (D-pad right and down on a controller), and it holds that speed with nothing pressed. Braking knocks it off.
  • The tractor carries a plough or a mower on its hitch (right click, or LB, swaps them). Lower it with Space (RB). The plough turns grass to dirt behind you and flings goblins aside; the mower makes short work of any goblin it catches. Both cut down flowers and slow the tractor while they work. An empty hitch lowered on a road scrapes and throws sparks.
  • Cars, vans, trucks and motorcycles now slide: where you're going only comes round to where you point as fast as the tyres grip, and they grip less on grass, sand, snow and ice. Steering eases off at speed, so changing lanes on the motorway is steadier. On the keyboard the throttle comes on over a moment rather than all at once.
  • Space (RB) is the handbrake. Hold it through a turn above about 25 km/h to drift: sparks fly off the back tyres as a charge builds in three stages, yellow, orange and red, shown round the speedometer. Let go for a boost of half a second, a second and a bit, or two seconds. Lorries, buses, trucks and fire engines are too heavy to drift: they just slide and slow down.
  • Right click (LB) is each vehicle's own special: a car flashes its headlights, dazzling goblins in front for a moment; a supercar burns nitro, which drifting fills back up; a motorcycle pulls a wheelie (with the throttle) or a stoppie; a four-wheeler or technical hops when you let go; a van, pickup, news van or motorhome puts its hazard lights on; a truck, lorry or bus works its air brakes; and a police car, ambulance or fire engine turns its siren on, and the traffic pulls over to let you by.

  • Hold C (click the right stick) to look behind you.
  • In the air, A and D still turn a car and W and S tip its nose, so you can straighten it up before you land.
  • Crash a motorcycle into a wall fast enough and you go flying off it.
  • The plane's throttle and the ship's engine order and rudder are now shown on their instruments rather than in the list of controls.
  • Planes fly properly in three dimensions now: roll right round, loop, fly upside down and knife-edge. Pull too hard when slow and the wing stalls, the nose drops and a wing goes down with it.

Auto picks Mouse-aim on a mouse and Direct on a controller. Let go of the stick in Direct and the wings come level and the plane holds its height.

  • Hold right click (LT) for the tank's gunsight: the view from the turret, zoomed in, with range marks. C (LB) is hull lock, holding the turret straight ahead so you can look round without it swinging.

  • Helicopters have weight: they tip into a move before the speed builds, and let go they flare nose-up and settle into a hover.
  • R (D-pad right) switches a helicopter to Agile flying: W and S are the collective (climb and sink), A and D the pedals (it turns on the spot, the camera turning with it), and pointing the camera tilts it, so you can strafe sideways and pull pedal turns. R again goes back to Simple.

  • Trains are driven with one lever: tap W for more power and S for more brake (five notches each way, or hold to sweep through them), and it stays where you leave it, so you can look round freely. A train carries its weight: it gathers speed slowly and takes real distance to stop. Space throws the emergency brake.
