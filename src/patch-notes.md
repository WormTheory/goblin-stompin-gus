# Patch notes

## Version 0.5

### Saves

- Your game now carries on just as you left it: your score, goblins slain and everything in This rampage, the time of day, the day and season, and the weather.
- Each player's health and stamina are kept too, and as a goblin your weapons, ammo, grenades and landmines. Left dead, you come back at full health. Player two gets theirs back on joining.
- Left in a car, tank, helicopter, plane, hot-air balloon or Mobile Gear, you're back in it, in the same seat, with the damage it had. Anything that was flying comes back on the ground below. Trains, boats and ships can't be brought back, so you start beside where you were.
- New city starts all of it afresh.

### Nuclear meltdown

- Bring down a reactor at a nuclear power station and the siren sounds. A few seconds later the whole place goes up: a blinding flash, a fireball and a shockwave that runs out across the town, flattening it as it goes. The middle is left a scorched crater ringed with rubble, the streets round it are levelled to the ground with a few stumps of wall standing, and further out buildings lose their top floors and the side that faced the blast, and some come down. Fires burn round the edge under a mushroom cloud.
- Anything caught in it is hurt by how close it was: near the middle there's nothing left of anyone, and further out you're knocked about. Cars, gas pumps and mines in the middle are simply gone rather than setting each other off.
- The game keeps running smoothly while it happens, and the crater is saved with the world and seen the same by everyone playing online.
- The Testing Environment has a Big blast button, with a slider for its size, to set one off ahead of you.
- The crater's rim of rubble now sits on the ground, so it no longer hangs in the air on uneven ground or where two craters overlap.
- Across the flattened town grass is burnt to dirt, streets are broken up in patches of rubble, and the buildings it brings down leave heaps of rubble behind.

### Nuke Launcher

- A new weapon for goblins, picked in the Loadout: a launcher with a nuclear warhead on the front. It lobs the warhead slowly in a shallow arc, and wherever it lands the same blast as a meltdown goes off, smaller but still enough to flatten a few streets.
- Fire it level and it lands far enough off for you to live through it. Fire it at a wall in front of you and you won't.
- One warhead in the tube and two spares, with a long reload. Once the spares are gone it's empty, unless Infinite ammo is on. The weapon bar shows how many spares are left.
- The debug menu has a Nuke strength slider to make its blast smaller or bigger, up to the size of a meltdown.

### Lighting

- At night, lights now light up what's around them. Street lamps pool light on the pavement, neon signs throw their own colours across the street, lit windows glow on the walls and rooms beside them, and lamps, TVs and screens light the rooms they're in. Walls block the light, so it stays in the room or the street it's in. Smash a lamp or a sign and its light goes out.
- It's switched on or off with Block lighting under Sound & picture in Options. Off, the game looks and runs exactly as before.
- A building that's been destroyed or brought down goes dark: its windows, neon and signs stop glowing, and the pieces of it falling don't glow either.
- In fog, rain and snow at night, lit windows, neon, lamps and the light they cast now show through the murk as a soft glow, some way past where the buildings themselves disappear.
- In thick fog the sky and clouds are now lost in the fog too, so far-off towers no longer stand out as dark shapes against a clear sky.
- The debug menu has a Fog light slider for how strongly lights show through fog.
- Fires and campfires now light up the ground and walls around them at night, dimming as they die down.
- In fog at night, street lamps, lamps, fires and traffic lights now have a soft halo of light in the air around them, as real fog scatters light. The Halos slider in the debug menu sets how strong they are.
- Traffic lights now light the junction under them at night in red, amber or green, changing with the lights.

### Trees and water in the distance

- Trees, ponds and lakes no longer pop up out of nowhere as you get close. Out past the full-detail city, the distant view now has every tree in its real spot, and up close each one becomes the real tree where it already stood.
- Further off, woods show as clumps of treetops, and ponds, park lakes and the theme park's boating lake show as water.
- Distant trees change colour with the seasons and lose their leaves in winter, like the near ones.

### Water

- Water now pours into holes. Knock out a block under water, or the wall holding it back, and the water flows in and fills the space below, up to its own level. Dig down through the sea bed or a river and the hole floods. It keeps pouring until the space is full, however far the tunnels or caves run, though a small pool only spills what it holds. The sea and rivers never drain.

### Crashes

- Vehicles now crash by their weight. A bus ploughs on through a car and shoves it aside, while a car that hits a bus mostly stops dead. Hit a car on its back corner and it spins round; traffic spun out stops steering for a moment, then sets off again.
- Hit hard enough side-on, a tall vehicle such as a van tips over on its side, and is righted a little later.
- Something much heavier can throw a lighter vehicle up over its bonnet. Bicycles and scooters hit at speed throw their riders off and cartwheel away.
- Damage depends on how hard the hit really was, so a gentle nudge does nothing and a big one hurts, more for the lighter of the two.
- Your own car spins when hit too, but you keep steering.
- Walls: a glancing blow turns you off along the wall rather than stopping you, and whether you smash through depends on your weight as well as your speed, so a bus goes through where a car bounces off.
- Gus and Gertie trample cars with their weight: a motorbike spins away, a bus barely moves.
- Trains, planes, boats and ships crash by their weight too. A ship ramming a boat hardly feels it while the boat takes the lot, and a car that hits a train or a plane is knocked back and spun round by it.
- A car wrecked at speed that slides into another car knocks it aside instead of stopping dead, and one spinning as it's wrecked keeps spinning as it slides.

### Bicycles and scooters

- Four light vehicles out in the streets, ridden by goblins going about their day and parked along the kerbs: a bicycle, a kick scooter, the Gobway (a self-balancing two-wheeler) and a motor scooter. Each is ridden its own way.
- Bicycle: hold forward to pedal along easily, or tap right click (controller RT) in a steady rhythm to pedal hard and go much faster. Hard pedalling tires your legs, shown on the bike computer, and they come back as you ease off. Space is the back brake, left click rings the bell.
- Kick scooter: tap forward to kick off, again and again to build speed, then coast. Hold it and you kick every so often. Press and let go of right click to ollie. No display: it's a kick scooter.
- Gobway: lean forward to go and back to slow and reverse. It turns on the spot when standing still. Push it past its top speed and it tips you back and beeps. Its handlebar display shows the battery, your lean and a light that turns yellow when it's pushing back and red when the battery's low (then it goes at half speed).
- Motor scooter: twist and go, quick off the line and easing off near the top. Right click flicks the indicators on toward the way you're steering, and again turns them off. Its round dial has a fuel gauge, and running dry leaves it spluttering along.
- They're light: hit a wall fast and you go over the bars.

### New vehicles

- Supercars: low, wide and very fast. Now and then one turns up in the traffic, tearing along the motorways well over the limit. Get in and it reaches over 60 a second, more than twice a car's top speed, so mind the walls. It steers a little less sharply the faster it goes.
- Forklifts: parked out in a factory's yard by the loading docks, between the lorries. Slow, steered by their back wheels.
- Tractors: parked on farms, one out in front of the barn and often another on the grass by the field, red on some farms and green on others. Get in and trundle off: slow, but they pull away steadily. Also in the debug menu's vehicle list.
- Hovercraft: rides over water just as it does over land, straight off the beach and out to sea, and slides round its turns. Debug menu only, for now.
- Fighter jet: flies like the airliner but far harder and faster, rolling and pulling hard and topping out at nearly twice the speed. Left click (controller RB) holds the cannon, right click (controller RT) fires a missile, homing on whatever you've locked on to with Q (controller R3). Debug menu only, for now.
- Hot-air balloon: Space (controller A) lights the burner and it rises, C (controller LB) vents it and it sinks, and left alone it cools and slowly settles. Moving nudges it gently the way you're looking. You can stand on top of the envelope. Shoot it and the envelope tears and the basket drops. Debug menu only, for now.
- All five are in the debug menu's vehicle list, and solid against every other vehicle like the rest.
- Airliners are bigger, about half as long again, to sit in proportion with the rest of the city's vehicles (about two-thirds of the real thing, as the cars, buses and tanks are). The fighter jet is in the same proportion, a little longer than a tank.

### Airports and airfields

- Airports have been rebuilt, bigger (16 by 8 blocks), and now sit just outside the big cities, one at most to a city, where one of its streets runs out of town straight to the terminal's drop-off road and car park.
- A terminal of six glass halls, each with check-in desks, a security checkpoint with metal detectors and bag scanners, and a departures lounge with its gate desk, coffee bar and seats looking out on the apron. Jet bridges reach out to the airliners parked at the gates. There's a control tower, three hangars, a fuel farm and a cargo shed.
- Security officers stand at each hall's checkpoint. They let you walk through, but hurt one and they all come for you.
- A runway with a parallel taxiway. Two airliners take turns flying: one taxis out, takes off and flies off over the horizon, the other comes in to land and taxis back to its stand, and neither moves while the other is using the runway.
- New: small country airfields out on the plains, with a grass strip, a windsock, a fuel pump, a hangar and a flying club's clubhouse (a desk, sofas, a briefing table and lockers, and a bathroom). Each has three two-seater planes: one flying circuits round the field, one tied down on the apron and one in the hangar.
- Two-seaters can be flown like the airliners: slower and lighter on the controls, they take off in a short run from grass and stall at much lower speeds.
- Every airport and airfield plane shows a pilot in the cockpit while it's flying or taxiing, and has a health bar.

### An aircraft carrier

- An aircraft carrier: 178 blocks long and 40 across its flight deck, about three-quarters the size of a real supercarrier. It gets a sea twice the size of the barge's, which grows away from you if a smaller one is already there.
- Every bit of it is real blocks you can walk through, fight in and smash, while it sails. From the top: the island on the starboard edge, with flight deck control, the flag bridge, the navigation bridge and Pri-Fly, the glassed-in top storey, then a roof with its mast; the flight deck, with the angled landing strip, its arresting wires and the catapult tracks painted on in thin lines like a highway's; the gallery deck under it, with ready rooms, the paraloft, the wardroom, catapult machinery, berths and stores; the hangar, one long bay split in three by open fire doors, with the elevator bays open to the sea; and the lower deck, with the mess deck, the galley, sick bay, the Goblin Intelligence Centre, berthing, the armoury, a glowing reactor room and the stores.
- It's furnished throughout with proper furniture rather than blocks, carried along with it as it sails: two-tier bunks and lockers in the berths, long tables and benches on the mess deck, fridges, ranges and sinks in the galley, beds, monitors and drips in the sick bay, desks and swivel chairs at the Intelligence Centre's screens, gun racks and ammo boxes in the armoury, sofas and tables in the lounges and the wardroom, rows of seats in the ready rooms, flight gear and kit racks in the paraloft, machinery, crates stacked in the stores, tool racks and fuel drums down the hangar's sides, and chairs on the island's bridges. It stays steady as the ship sails. Goblins go round it, but Gus and Gertie knock it flying as they barge through, and stomps, blasts and crashes topple it like any other furniture; smash the deck out from under something and it goes.
- Every doorway has a steel door that swings open as you or a goblin come up to it and closes behind you: each room's door off the corridors, the corridors' doors through the bulkheads, the stern door, the hangar's forward door and the island's door on to the flight deck.
- Swim to the stern and climb out on to the landing under the flight deck's overhang, then go in by the stern door. Stairs run between every deck, fore and aft, up through hatches on to the flight deck, and up through the island to its roof. Goblins chase you through it all, up and down the stairs.
- As Gnash or Grizzle, take the helm on the navigation bridge with F (controller Y) and drive it like a ship:
  - W and S ring the engine telegraph up and down its orders, one a press, with a bell: Back full, Back 1/3, Stop, Ahead 1/3, Ahead 2/3, Ahead full and Flank. It gathers way slowly toward the order, taking about forty seconds to work up to flank, 32 knots, and about thirty to stop again.
  - A and D put the rudder over a little at a time, and it stays where you leave it. The turn builds up slowly and keeps going while the rudder's over. C brings the rudder back amidships. The rudder only bites while it's moving.
  - The display shows the order, the speed in knots and where the rudder is.
  - The camera hangs well back and high, to take in the whole ship. R switches to the view from the bridge, through its windows, and back.
  - The engines keep to the last order when you let go of the helm, so you can set it going and walk about while it sails.
  - Run it into water too shallow for it and it runs aground: it grinds to a stop with a shudder, takes damage by how fast it hit (about a sixteenth of its health at flank), and its engines are rung down to Stop. Ring astern to back off.
  - It rams what it runs into: a boat, or the barge, takes five times the knock, and the carrier hardly feels it.
  - Bite sounds the horn.
- Vehicles can go aboard. Spawn cars, tanks and helicopters from the debug menu while you're on the flight deck and they're parked on it; they ride along as it sails, can be driven about the deck, and fly or drive off its edge into the world. Fly a helicopter over the deck and let it down to land on it. A tank's shells and a helicopter's guns aim where you look from aboard and fly on out over the side. (A Mobile Gear spawned there stands on the deck too, and you can climb into it from the deck; planes and balloons are put out in the world beside it.)
- Gnash's guns and grenades aim properly from aboard.
- Sailors and marines drop their rifles when Gnash or Grizzle kills them (marines drop grenades too), as preppers, militiamen and soldiers do. Before, they dropped nothing.
- Weapons and grenades dropped aboard the carrier or the barge show on the deck where the goblin fell. Before, they were left out of sight, so nothing seemed to drop.
- It has a crew now. Deck crew in coloured jerseys (yellow, green, blue, red, purple, white and brown, with helmets to match) work the flight deck, sailors go about the hangar, the gallery deck and the lower deck, and marines stand watch on the flight deck, the bridge and below. They come out as you get near and stroll between their spots while it's quiet.
- Board it, bring a vehicle in close, or hurt one of them and it goes to general quarters: a klaxon sounds, the deck crew scatter, marines and sailors hunt you through the ship with their rifles (marines throw grenades too), and a sailor runs to each deck weapon and opens up on you. It stands down when you've been well away for half a minute. In Peaceful mode they leave you be.
- Six deck weapons stand on the flight deck's edges, two of each kind, and they fire on whatever comes at the ship: rockets and missiles first, then aircraft, then vehicles, then you on foot (the Sea Sparrow only bothers with vehicles and aircraft). They don't fire at anything on the ship itself.
  - The CIWS (Phalanx) spins up and pours out a fast stream of rounds, and shoots rockets and missiles out of the air. It overheats if you hold it down too long.
  - The Sea Sparrow launcher fires big, slow-turning homing missiles that hit hard, eight to a load. Lock on first.
  - The RAM launcher fires small, quick missiles at whatever's nearest the crosshair, 21 to a load.
  - The Sea Sparrow and RAM launchers have proper models now. The Sea Sparrow is a turntable with two arms holding a pair of white four-cell launcher boxes, and the RAM is a squat launcher with a 21-cell face and seeker pods. The covers on their cells come off one by one as they fire, and they're all back on once it's reloaded.
- Any goblin can take a deck weapon's seat with F (controller Y), crewed or not: whoever was manning it is thrown off, angry, and the ship goes to general quarters. Bite fires.
- Sailors, marines and deck crew are also in the debug menu's goblin list.
- It can be sunk, but only by wrecking its hull: the decks, the island and the flight deck can be smashed to pieces without sinking it. Its health is how much of its hull is left. Cut right through the hull anywhere (even a narrow cut will do, as long as it goes all the way through) and the ship snaps in two there, with a blast all along the break and a long groan of steel. The shorter end breaks off and goes down while the rest stays afloat, and you can do it again and again. The piece that's going stays level for a while, so you can walk across on to it from the rest, or jump or climb on to it, and ride it down until the water reaches you. After a few seconds it drifts away from the rest, and once nobody's on it, its far end tips under and it's gone. Lose too much of the hull altogether and the rest goes down too. Shells, rockets and blasts from outside count by the holes they blow, and the hit itself does nothing, and neither does running aground. (The barge still takes damage from hits as before.)
- Bullets work across its side now: rounds from outside (a helicopter's gun, the Mobile Gear's rifle on its deck, Gnash's rifle from the shore) break its blocks and hit the goblins aboard, and rounds fired from aboard hit what's out in the world past the side.
- The Mobile Gear's Heat Cleaver works on the carrier now: it cuts through its blocks, and through goblins and vehicles aboard, whether you're stood on its deck or swinging at it from alongside.
- A RAM or Sea Sparrow missile you fire from one of its deck weapons can hit the carrier itself now. (The crew's missiles still never touch it.)
- Its deck weapons are targets in their own right, like small vehicles bolted to the deck: lock on to them from a vehicle or the Mobile Gear, and shoot, blast or ram them. Each has its own health; wrecked, it goes up in a blast and smoke, its gunner is thrown off, and it's left a blackened, lopsided wreck that nobody can man.
- Eight fighter jets are parked on the flight deck, along the starboard side and the port bow.
  - Climb into one with F (controller Y) and fly it like any jet.
  - Catapults: taxi on to the start of one of the four tracks (two at the bow, two in the waist) and stop there, and it hooks on. Open the throttle all the way and it holds you for a moment, then flings you down the track and off the bow at flying speed.
  - Landing: press R (the weapon button) to lower the hook (any jet has one, so a jet from the debug menu can land on it too), and come in low and slow over the stern along the angled strip. Catch one of the four wires and you're pulled up in a few lengths. Miss them (or forget the hook) and you'll need full power to go round again before the deck runs out.
  - They ride along with the ship while it sails, and they're carried into the world when you fly off.
- At general quarters, pilots run to the parked jets. Each jet is towed to a free catapult and launched, and it goes after you: cannon passes up close and missiles from further off. After a while, or out of missiles, it flies round behind the stern, lines up on the angled strip, comes down the glide slope with its hook down and catches a wire. Then it's towed back to its spot, and its pilot heads below. A jet that misses the wires goes round and tries again. The carrier's own anti-missile guns leave its jets' missiles alone, and its jets don't run into each other on the deck.
- Out in the world now, not just the Testing Environment: one lies at anchor in open water off each naval base, crewed, armed and with its jets on deck, ready for you to swim out and board. In the Testing Environment it's still in the debug menu's vehicle list, on a sea of its own.
- Each one remembers everything. Leave it (sailed off somewhere, holed, its deck weapons wrecked, its jets moved or shot down, its furniture knocked about, broken in two) and it's just as you left it when you come back, after reloading the game too. Sink it and it's gone for good. Jets its own pilots were flying when you left are back on deck; one you flew off and left somewhere else is gone.
- Online, everyone sees the same carrier, as remembered, including a player who joins later.

### A barge you can walk around (Testing Environment)

- The first step toward an aircraft carrier: a test barge, in the debug menu's vehicle list (Testing Environment only for now). Spawning it lays out a square of deep sea ahead of you, if there isn't one yet, and floats the barge on it.
- It's built of real blocks, like a building, so everything works aboard it while it sails and turns: walking, jumping, climbing, fighting, and smashing holes in its deck. A wheelhouse at the stern has steel stairs up to its roof, a rail runs round the deck, and crates are stacked on the foredeck.
- Swim out and climb aboard up the steps of the swim platform at the stern, or jump down on to the deck from above. Swim away from it and you're back in the open sea.
- As Gnash or Grizzle, stand at the helm inside the wheelhouse's front window and press F (controller Y) to take the controls: it's driven the same way as the aircraft carrier, with the engine telegraph and the rudder, but it's much quicker to speed up, slow down and turn.
- Goblins aboard chase you round the deck as anywhere else, and goblins in the sea swim out to it and climb aboard after you.
- Online co-op: everyone in the game sees the barge (and the aircraft carrier) where the host has it, and can climb aboard, walk about on it, stand at the helm and sail it, smash holes in it and go down with it. On the aircraft carrier everyone sees the deck weapons turn, fire and get wrecked, and can man one; the jets taxi, launch and land; furniture knocked over aboard falls for everyone; and when the carrier breaks in two, everyone sees the same break and the end that sinks. The bridge view at the helm works for everyone too.

### Mobile Gear (Testing Environment)

- A new vehicle to try out in the Testing Environment: the Mobile Gear, an armoured walker eight blocks tall. Pick it from the vehicle buttons in the Testing Environment tab and it's set down in front of you. Only Gnash and Grizzle can pilot it for now.
- Walk up and press F (controller Y) to climb in. Hold F for half a second to climb out, so a stray tap won't throw you out mid-fight.
- It moves where the camera points, with the view from behind and above it so the crosshair sits in the middle of the screen and you can see over its head. On foot it sets off slowly and builds up into a jog, its strides lengthening and its arms swinging, stepping whichever way you're going, backwards and sideways included. Standing still, its legs stay planted as you look around until its body has turned too far on them, then they step round to catch up. Press C (controller B) to switch boost on and it drops into a wide, braced crouch and skates along the ground three times as fast, at no cost. Everything is about a fifth faster than it was.
- Shift (controller X) is a quick boost: a sharp burst in the direction you're holding, or straight back with nothing held, taken in the same braced skating crouch as boosting, then easing off smoothly rather than stopping dead. Each one costs energy.
- Space (controller A) jumps: it dips into a quick crouch, pushes off, tucks its legs up and swings them out beneath it. Holding it flies upward on the thrusters. In the air it leans well into the way it's flying, its legs hang nearly straight, swept back behind it the faster it goes, and its back and calf thrusters tip down to hold it up. With boost on in the air it glides instead of dropping.
- Thrusters run on the energy gauge, a thin bar right across the bottom of the lock box round the crosshair. Empty it and it redlines: the bar flashes red and you can't boost or fly until it recovers. Energy comes back quickly once you stop drawing on it, and faster on the ground.
- It leans into the way it's going, and its thrusters fire from whichever side pushes it there: the back ones going forward, the side ones strafing, the chest ones going backwards. Its legs fold up smoothly as it leaves the ground and its feet stay planted flat, up and down steps too. Skating over a pit, its feet ease over the gap rather than dropping to the bottom. Its thruster flames taper to a point.
- It steps up one-block ledges on foot and two-block ledges while boosting. Run into a wall or a building while boosting and it smashes a hole and carries on; at full boost it ploughs straight through with barely a stumble. Walking into one just stops it. Anything small in its way gets trampled, and a hard landing knocks goblins over.
- It's armed, with a weapon on each trigger. Each has its own load and reloads by itself once it's empty. Above the speedometer, in the bottom corner, each weapon's button, what it has loaded and its name are laid out as they're carried (shoulders above, arms below), in a panel that keeps the same size whatever it says, and up either side of the lock box round the crosshair a tall gauge for each weapon shows how full it is, or how far along its reload is. The gauges and the energy bar are see-through, so they don't hide what you're aiming at:
  - Right click (controller RB): the Rivet Rifle in its right hand. Hold it for a steady stream of rounds. Its rounds chip at walls: about half the time a round breaks the block it hits.
  - Left click (controller LB): the Heat Cleaver on its left arm. It lunges forward (at a locked target if one's close, ending with it in reach), easing smoothly off rather than halting, then swings. The lunge adds to however you're already moving, so walking or boosting you keep going through the attack. It reaches across to its right side, its whole upper body turning with it and its right arm lifting out of the way, the blade ignites from its left hand, a flat blade held level, and it whips the blade from right to left in a wide, level arc round in front of it that cuts down goblins, tears into vehicles and slices clean through walls. The swing follows your aim: locked on to something low, or looking down, it bends forward at the hips and slices downward, so it can reach goblins below it and cut into the ground; aimed high it leans back and slices upward. The blade retracts into its hand as it settles back. It needs a moment to cool between swings.
  - Q (controller LT): the Swarm Pod on its left shoulder. Hold it and the pod locks on to whatever's in the lock box, up to four locks, then let go and a homing missile flies at each. With nothing locked, a tap fires one where you're aiming.
  - E (controller RT): the Breaker Cannon on its right shoulder. On the ground it stops, braces and swings the cannon over its shoulder before firing a heavy shell that blasts a crater. In the air it fires at once and the kick shoves it backwards. It sits up on a post, so firing low it clears the Gear's own chest.
- Lock-on: anything in the box round the crosshair is picked out by itself, and the rifle and blade aim at it, leading a moving target. Middle click (controller R3) holds on to it with a hard lock, and the camera turns to keep it in view while you move round it. Press it again to let go.
- It has 600 health. Wreck it and you're thrown out.
- X (controller L3) is the assault boost. It crouches and glows for a moment with a rising whine, then dashes off fast straight where you're aiming, up or down too, turning only slowly after your aim. Its weapons still fire on the way. It costs a chunk of energy to start and keeps drawing on it, and it smashes straight through walls. Press it again, or pull back, to stop.
- Press it again near the target you're locked on to and it flies at it with a kick instead: not much damage, but it'll nearly always stagger what it hits.
- Staggering: every hit fills a target's impact meter, which drains again once the hits stop. A full meter staggers it for 2 seconds: it can't move or shoot, and takes half again as much damage. Heavy blows (the kick, the Breaker Cannon, the Heat Cleaver) fill it faster. Tanks, helicopters and the Mobile Gear itself can all be staggered.
- Your own impact meter is the thinner bar under the energy bar, with STAGGERED flashing under it while you are. A locked tank or helicopter shows its health and its impact meter under its bracket.
- R (controller D-pad down) uses a repair kit, putting back 35% of its health over a second. Each Mobile Gear carries three, and the controls list on the left says how many are left.
- Co-op: each player can climb into a Mobile Gear of their own, and two of them bump into each other rather than walking through.
- Online: a guest sees the host's Mobile Gears move, stride, fire their thrusters and swing their weapons, and can climb in and pilot one, with its own display.
- Redlined, its thrusters cough and sputter until the energy comes back.
- Its thrusters make a steady roar while they burn, instead of a string of clicks. It's quiet, and swells and dies away with the flames.
- It gets going quicker from a standstill, and no longer gets stuck walking slowly into a step or a ledge (it used to end up wedged in it, and only a jump or a quick boost got it free).
- Its lock-on picks out tanks, helicopters, an aircraft carrier's deck weapons and its jets in the air twice as far off as goblins, and holds a lock on them that far too.
- Pick its loadout in the Testing Environment tab, under "Mobile Gear loadout": each of its four weapon slots has a second choice. The left arm can carry a second Rivet Rifle instead of the Heat Cleaver; the right arm a Scrap Shotgun (a spread of pellets, short-ranged and hard-hitting); the left shoulder a Rocket Volley (a stream of unguided rockets, no locking needed); and the right shoulder a Clod Lobber (shells lobbed high on to whatever you aim at, more of them than the cannon and no bracing). Your choice is remembered, and only Mobile Gears spawned after it carry it.
- Its controls can be rebound in Options, in their own "Piloting a Mobile Gear" section. The weapon buttons are now named by slot (left arm, right shoulder and so on), since the loadout decides what's in each.
- Mobile Gears aren't kept when you leave the Testing Environment.
- While you're piloting, the controls line for on foot is hidden.
- Getting into any vehicle or gun seat no longer pops up a card of its controls on the right: they're all in the list down the left, including a helicopter's climb and drop, flying a plane, and the fire buttons.
- The Mobile Gear is now solid against boats (it used to walk straight through them) and every other vehicle, and can stand on any of them: a boat, a train, a plane, a tank, another Mobile Gear. Stood on something that moves, it's carried along.
- Its guns now fire from the ends of their barrels wherever the arms happen to be pointing, and the arms swing round smoothly on to what they're aiming at instead of snapping between positions.
- Its thrusters are a little louder.
- Every vehicle can now be driven. Subway trains: climb in at a station and drive one round its loop, forwards or back. A rowboat pulled up on the beach, or a ship in a dry dock, is pushed off into the nearest open water as you climb in.

### Goblin loadout

- Playing as Gnash or Grizzle, the Character tab now shows a Loadout in place of the hippos' abilities: pick which weapons he starts with (club, dagger, handgun, rifle) and how many grenades, from none to 9.
- At first it's as before: just the club and no grenades. He always keeps at least one weapon, and anything else you can still take off the enemies you kill.
- Each player has their own loadout, kept between visits, and in split screen player two picks theirs from their own pause menu. Gnash and Grizzle share it, as they share everything else.
- Changing it while playing re-arms your goblin at once.
- New weapon: the Fireball Spellbook. Cultists carry it now, an open book in one hand, and conjure their fireballs out of the other. Kill one as Gnash or Grizzle and it drops the book; pick it up, or choose it in the Loadout. Once you have the book, cultists drop their ritual dagger instead. Left click (controller RB) conjures a fireball in your free hand for a moment, then hurls it where you're aiming, bending gently after the nearest enemy (or another player, with PvP on). It's slow to come again, about three seconds, and does the same damage as a cultist's.
- An enemy you kill now drops the first weapon it carries that you haven't got yet, rather than nothing once you have its first one.
- Fireballs now burst on a vehicle in their way and damage it, as bullets do, instead of flying straight through.

### New weapons and reloading

- Seven new weapons for Gnash and Grizzle, each in the Loadout and dropped by any enemy that carries one:
  - Katana: a quick, wide cut from high on the right down across to the left, wide enough to catch two goblins side by side.
  - Large Club: heaved up over the shoulder, held at the top for a beat, then slammed into the ground in front of you. Slow, but it hits hard, dents cars badly and shakes the ground.
  - Shotgun: eight pellets a shot, brutal close up and little use far off. The pump racks back and forward after every shot.
  - Assault Rifle: hold the button and it keeps firing. The view climbs a little with each shot, so pull it back down.
  - Sniper Rifle: one heavy round at a time, from much further than anything else, with the bolt worked between shots. Hold right click (controller RT) to look down its scope: the view narrows right in, aiming slows to match, and the round goes exactly where the crosshair is. Fired without the scope, it strays a little off the middle. With the sniper in hand, right click is the scope rather than a grenade.
  - Bazooka: a rocket off the shoulder that bursts on whatever it meets, goblins, vehicles or walls, knocking out a crater. One shot, then a slow reload.
  - Landmines: carried as a pile, like grenades (pick how many in the Loadout, up to 6). Hold them in hand and attack to crouch and press one into the ground just ahead. It arms after a second, blinking red, then goes off under the first enemy, vehicle or player over it, you too once you've stepped off it.
- Landmines can be set off on purpose, armed or not: shoot one, hit it with any attack (a club, a bite, a stomp, a roll), catch it in a blast or a fireball, or drive over it in anything from a car to a tank, a landed helicopter or a Mobile Gear. A mine going off sets off any others in its blast.
- Guns now have magazines and reload. The handgun holds 8, the rifle 5, the shotgun 6, the assault rifle 30, the sniper rifle 5 and the bazooka 1. X (controller D-pad down) reloads; firing the last round, or attacking with it empty, reloads by itself. Switching weapons stops a reload, and each gun keeps what's left in it. The weapon bar shows the rounds left and when it's reloading.
- Reloads are animated in your own view (the magazine dropped out and a new one slapped in, shells pushed in one by one, the rocket slid into the tube) and on your goblin as the others see him.
- Enemies reload too: police officers, militia, preppers, soldiers, sailors and marines stop to change magazines once they've emptied their guns. One that also carries a blade or club drops the reload to fight you if you come close.
- None of the enemies carry the new weapons yet, but any of them can: an assault rifle is fired in bursts of four, a shotgun's pellets and a bazooka's rockets come at you as they do from yours, and one carrying landmines kneels to plant them in your path.
- Melee weapons now each have their own wind-up and follow-through on the enemies who swing them, so a heavy weapon's blow is slower to come and slower to recover from.

### PvP

- A new PvP switch in Options, off unless you turn it on. With it on, players can fight each other, in split screen or online, whoever they're playing as.
- Lock on to another player as you would a goblin, on foot, from a tank or helicopter, or with a Mobile Gear's lock box, and its missiles home in on them. A player in a vehicle is locked on as the vehicle.
- Every attack lands on other players: bites and uppercuts, stomps, ground pounds, ground spikes, trampling, rolls, dashes, blast dashes and blast long jumps, and Gnash's club, dagger, guns and grenades. A hippo's blows hit another player for about a third of what they do to a goblin, so a fight lasts a while.
- Vehicles too: run another player down in a car or a tank, shoot them with a tank's or helicopter's guns, shells and rockets, or with a Mobile Gear's rifle, blade, missiles and cannon (it tramples them as well).
- A player killed by another is told who did it, the winner scores 500 points, and the obituaries name the killer. You get up again as usual.
- Online, the host's switch decides for everyone. A guest's PvP switch shows the host's choice and can't be changed.

### Vehicle controls

- In every vehicle, its controls are listed down the left side of the screen, one to a line, instead of along the bottom. The controls line for on foot is hidden while you drive.
- Every kind of vehicle has its own set of controls, each with its own section in Controls to rebind: cars and trucks, the forklift, the tractor, the tank, the helicopter, planes, boats and ships, trains and the hot-air balloon, as the Mobile Gear already had. Keys only clash within the same set, so the same key can do different things in different vehicles. Steering and the throttle are still the walking keys and the left stick; Get in / out, Lock on and the online chat keys work everywhere.
- On a controller, the triggers are now an analog throttle and brake in cars: squeeze RT to go, LT to brake and reverse.
- Planes: on the keyboard the throttle is now Left Shift and C (Space is the brakes); on a controller RT and LT. Lock on in a plane is the middle mouse button (R3), so Q and E are free for the rudder.
- Tanks fire with RT on a controller, helicopters fire their gun with RT and rockets with LT, and LB and RB descend and climb.
- Options: the Speedometer switch is now Vehicle instruments.
- Every vehicle has instruments of its own in the bottom-right corner in place of the one speedometer: a dashboard with a rev counter for cars, a digital display that shows the lean on motorcycles, a truck dial with an air-pressure gauge, a fan and slip gauge on the hovercraft, the mast height, tilt and tip risk on the forklift, the gear range and implement on the tractor, a turret and hull compass on the tank, an artificial horizon with height and airspeed on the helicopter, round dials in the two-seater, a glass cockpit display in the airliner, an engine telegraph and rudder on boats and ships, oars on the rowing boat, a brake and power lever on trains and the wind at each height in the balloon. The fighter jet's display is drawn over the whole view, like a real head-up display.
- Speeds are in km/h on land and knots on the water and in the air.
- The forklift's forks work. Hold Space to raise them and C to lower them (RB and LB on a controller), and hold Left Shift (X) with either to tilt the mast. Raise them under a crate, a goblin or a light car and it comes up with you. Lower it on to anything, a ledge, a roof or the ground, and it's set down there. Drive at a goblin with the forks up and they spear it. The forklift steers with its back wheels, so it turns almost on the spot with its tail swinging wide, and it beeps when it reverses. Take a sharp turn with a load held high and it tips over on its side, throwing you out; get back in to rock it back on to its wheels.
- Lifting a car on the forklift no longer sends it sliding backwards: the car and the forklift were shoving each other apart every moment. A car on the forks now stays put and the forklift drives off with it.
- The forklift can lift players too. Stand on the forks, as Gus, Gertie, Gnash or Grizzle, while someone raises them and up you go, carried along as it drives. The instruments show who's on board (a hippo weighs more than a car, so mind the tip risk). Step or jump off whenever you like.
- Theme park rides and zoo animals have health bars (Options' Health bars), like every other vehicle: each coaster car, the Ferris wheel and each of its gondolas, the carousel, and every animal.
- The tractor has a hand throttle: R steps it up through low, middle and high, Left Shift steps it down (D-pad right and down on a controller), and it holds that speed with nothing pressed. Braking knocks it off.
- The tractor carries a plough or a mower on its hitch (right click, or LB, swaps them). Lower it with Space (RB). The plough turns grass to dirt behind you and flings goblins aside; the mower makes short work of any goblin it catches. Both cut down flowers and slow the tractor while they work. An empty hitch lowered on a road scrapes and throws sparks.
- Cars, vans, trucks and motorcycles now slide: where you're going only comes round to where you point as fast as the tyres grip, and they grip less on grass, sand, snow and ice. Steering eases off at speed, so changing lanes on the motorway is steadier. On the keyboard the throttle comes on over a moment rather than all at once.
- Space (RB) is the handbrake. Hold it through a turn above about 25 km/h to drift: sparks fly off the back tyres as a charge builds in three stages, yellow, orange and red, shown round the speedometer. Let go for a boost of half a second, a second and a bit, or two seconds. Lorries, buses, trucks and fire engines are too heavy to drift: they just slide and slow down.
- Right click (LB) is each vehicle's own special: a car flashes its headlights, dazzling goblins in front for a moment; a supercar burns nitro, which drifting fills back up; a motorcycle pulls a wheelie (with the throttle) or a stoppie; a four-wheeler or technical hops when you let go; a van, pickup, news van or motorhome puts its hazard lights on; a truck, lorry or bus works its air brakes; and a police car, ambulance or fire engine turns its siren on, and the traffic pulls over to let you by.
- Supercar launch control: hold the handbrake and the throttle at a standstill, then let the handbrake go for a perfect start.
- Hold C (click the right stick) to look behind you.
- In the air, A and D still turn a car and W and S tip its nose, so you can straighten it up before you land.
- Crash a motorcycle into a wall fast enough and you go flying off it.
- The plane's throttle and the ship's engine order and rudder are now shown on their instruments rather than in the list of controls.
- Planes fly properly in three dimensions now: roll right round, loop, fly upside down and knife-edge. Pull too hard when slow and the wing stalls, the nose drops and a wing goes down with it.
- Two ways to fly, picked in Options under Plane controls: Mouse-aim (point the camera where you want to go and the plane rolls and pulls itself there, keeping out of stalls and off the ground; W and S are the throttle, A and D roll by hand, hold X to look round without turning) and Direct (W and S pitch, A and D roll, Left Shift and C the throttle, Q and E the rudder; on a controller the left stick, RT and LT the throttle, LB and RB the rudder). Auto picks Mouse-aim on a mouse and Direct on a controller. Let go of the stick in Direct and the wings come level and the plane holds its height.
- Space is the wheel brakes on the ground and the airbrake in the air. R raises and lowers the undercarriage (the jet's R is still its hook). Land with the gear up and you slide in on the belly: you'll walk away, but that plane won't fly again.
- The two-seater rolls quickly and loops easily, and right click turns on a smoke trail for skywriting.
- The airliner is heavy and turns wide. Right click switches on the autopilot, which holds your height and heading (with a chime); touch the stick and it lets go. Pull it too hard and the wings creak, then start to give.
- The fighter jet rolls fast and holds its speed through turns. Hold Left Shift at full throttle for the afterburner, until it overheats; tap A or D twice for a barrel roll; pull hard and the edges of the view grey out.
- Tanks are heavy now: they take a moment to get going and to stop. A and D turn them on their tracks, spinning on the spot when standing still, with the turns widening as they pick up speed.
- Left Shift (controller left stick click) steps the tank's cruise through slow, half and full, and it keeps going at that speed with nothing pressed. S knocks it off.
- The tank's aim spreads while it moves or the turret swings, shown by the crosshair growing, and settles about a second after it stops. A ring round the crosshair fills as the gun reloads.
- R (D-pad right) swaps the tank's shells: HE blows a wide crater, AP punches a narrow tunnel deep through walls and does twice the damage to vehicles.
- Hold right click (LT) for the tank's gunsight: the view from the turret, zoomed in, with range marks. C (LB) is hull lock, holding the turret straight ahead so you can look round without it swinging.
- A tank's hull tilts with the ground it's on, over ramps and rubble.
- Helicopters have weight: they tip into a move before the speed builds, and let go they flare nose-up and settle into a hover.
- R (D-pad right) switches a helicopter to Agile flying: W and S are the collective (climb and sink), A and D the pedals (it turns on the spot, the camera turning with it), and pointing the camera tilts it, so you can strafe sideways and pull pedal turns. R again goes back to Simple.
- Starting a helicopter, the rotor spools up with a rising whine before it'll lift off.
- Brought down to a quarter of its health, a helicopter's engine fails and it autorotates: it sinks, turning slowly, but you can still steer it, and holding Space near the ground flares it into a rough landing you'll survive if you aren't coming down too fast. The instrument shows ENGINE OUT.
- The main rotor is a weapon: brush it against goblins on a roof and it minces them. Clip a wall and it chips blocks out, but it hurts the helicopter too.
- A boat's throttle has notches now: tap W or S to step between astern, stop, slow, half and full (or hold to sweep through them), and it stays where you leave it while you look round. The triggers step it on a controller.
- Boats ride waves that grow with the wind, and lean into their turns. Speedboats and launches plane at speed: the bow lifts, they go faster, and cresting a wave throws them into the air.
- Space drops the anchor: under way, the boat stops hard and swings round on it. The aircraft carrier and the barge have an anchor too.
- The rowboat is rowed: left and right click pull each oar. Both together go straight, one alone turns it, and a steady rhythm goes faster. The pedalo is pedalled with W (faster tapped in rhythm) and steered with A and D.
- Right click is each boat's own special: a speedboat's boost, a launch's or ferry's horn blast that scatters goblins, the sailboat's boom swinging across to knock goblins overboard, the trawler's net hauling in swimming goblins, the tug's push mode for shoving moored boats about, the patrol boat's deck gun and the pedalo's bell.
- There's a wind now, slowly wandering round and stronger in storms. The sailboat goes fastest across it.
- Trains are driven with one lever: tap W for more power and S for more brake (five notches each way, or hold to sweep through them), and it stays where you leave it, so you can look round freely. A train carries its weight: it gathers speed slowly and takes real distance to stop. Space throws the emergency brake.
- The reverser (R) sets forward, neutral or reverse, but only once the train has stopped.
- Every bend has a safe speed, and the instrument shows the next one coming up. Take it too fast and the wheels squeal; far too fast and the train comes off the rails.
- Left click sounds the horn (hold it for a long blast) and right click rings the bell. Subway trains use the same lever.
- The hot-air balloon is steered by the wind now. There are four layers of it, by height (surface, low, middle and high), each blowing its own way and harder the higher you go: climb or sink into the one going where you want. Its instrument shows each layer's direction and which one you're in. The movement keys only trim the drift a little.
- The burner warms the envelope a moment behind, so start a climb early.
- Right click drops a sandbag: the balloon lifts a little, and the bag flattens any goblin it lands on. You have six.
- Left click lets down a rope. Hostile goblins grab it and climb up (three at most), weighing the balloon down, and any that reach the basket tear at it. Pull the rope up to shake off the ones still climbing. Another player can climb the rope too, holding climb.
- The Mobile Gear has its own instrument: a futuristic speed ring, its segments filling with speed, with a tag for what it's doing (walking, skating, in the air, assault boosting, or redlined with its energy gone), its climb rate, its height and a pip for each quick boost its energy will pay for.

### Wrecks keep going

- A vehicle destroyed on the move no longer stops dead. Its wreck skids, slides or falls on the way it was going, and the faster and heavier it was, the further it goes: a bus skids further than a car, a motorbike less.
- Shot down in the air, a helicopter, plane or hot-air balloon arcs down to the ground rather than dropping straight.
- A wreck ploughs through what's in its way, smashing the blocks as it goes. Glass and fences hardly slow it, brick and concrete take more, steel more still, and bedrock stops it flat. A plane brought down at speed into an office block ends up buried halfway into it; a car into a wall pushes in a block or so.
- A wrecked car now leaves its burnt-out shell lying where it ends up for a while, instead of vanishing on the spot.
- A wreck always comes to rest on whatever's under it, and if that's blown away later, down it drops. Anything carried on a wrecked forklift's forks comes down too.
- A train that loses a car grinds to a stop over a longer distance, as befits something that heavy.

### Pilots watch where they're going

- The aircraft carrier's fighter jets look ahead now and pull up or bank away from what's in front of them: the carrier's island, buildings, hills. Pulling away after a pass, they turn toward open sky instead of straight on into whatever's there.
- The military bases' helicopters climb over buildings and hills in their way instead of flying through them.
- They're not perfect: each pilot has their own skill, and now and then one reacts too late or doesn't see it, so you'll still see the odd jet hit the island.

### Map markers

Click the pin again to take it away, or somewhere else to move it.

- The map is smooth to pan and zoom however much of the world you've explored. Zoomed right out, it can take a moment to fill in the first time.

### Enemy hostility: Pacifist

### Debug menu

- **Grenades** stack, up to 9. Soldiers, preppers and militia drop them, and Gnash throws one with stomp (right click, RT). Pipe bombs are gone: preppers throw grenades too.
- The controls hint shows Gnash's own moves while you play as him.
- **Mixed co-op:** player two can now be a hippo or a goblin, whoever player one picked. Choose it in the "Co-op, player two" row under the character cards: Gus and Gnash can play together, each with their own moves, camera and controls hint.

### Goblins and enemies

- Goblins chasing Gus up a wall climb after him, and club him once they're level instead of waiting below.
- Falls of more than 6 blocks hurt goblins, a point for every 2 blocks more, so a 16-block drop kills one. Water breaks the fall, and their routes no longer jump down drops that would hurt.
- **Enemy hostility** is now a slider on the main screen and in the quick debug menu. Passive: nobody reacts to you, even when hit. Peaceful: nobody attacks, but people still run. Normal: they come for you. An old Peaceful setting carries over.
- Playing as a goblin no longer calms everyone down. Hostility works just as it does for Gus.
- **Crews:** a base's tanks and helicopters and a militia camp's vehicles now start empty. When the camp is set off, soldiers run out of the barracks and militiamen out of the HQ, climb aboard, and only then do the vehicles start up. Shoot one on the way and its vehicle waits for the next. When a crewed vehicle is destroyed, its crew's bodies are thrown out.

### Vehicles

- **Carjacking:** playing as Gnash, getting into a vehicle that has someone at the controls hauls them out first. A car's door swings open, a motorcycle's rider is shoved off, a tank's crewman is dragged out of the hatch and a helicopter's door slides back. Whoever's thrown out, a driver, a police officer or a soldier, comes back at you or runs for it.
- All vehicles now collide with each other. Tanks no longer drive through a helicopter's tail, trains, planes, boats and rides push tanks and cars out of the way, and driven planes and boats stop or crash when they meet another vehicle.
- A driven tank ramming a wall knocks out a hole as big as its front and keeps going at speed. It also fits under overhangs it can pass beneath.
- Firing with nothing loaded just clicks, and the tank's last shell marker empties while it reloads.
- The vehicle health box and ammo panel now sit just above the hearts.
- The get-out prompt shows Xbox button labels on Xbox controllers.
- Parked motorcycles no longer have a rider, and a ridden one's rider sits properly on it.
- **Planes fly properly:** a driven plane has throttle, banks to turn, pitches up and down, gets its lift from airspeed, and stalls if it flies too slowly.
- **Fire engines** have a working water cannon.
- **Militia camps** have two technicals (pickups with a machine gun and a gunner in the back) and two four-wheelers (a rider and a rifleman behind him). Each carries a driver and a gunner. Once crewed they circle you while the gunner fires. Gnash can carjack them, and the gunner jumps off to fight. The Testing Environment has buttons for both.

### Movement

- Air dash comes back after Gus leaves the water, and can't be used while swimming.
- Holding jump keeps you swimming up.
- **Fighting from a wall:** while spider-climbing a wall or the side of a vehicle, R1 (left click) snaps at goblins the way the hippo's head points: up, along or down. It bites vehicles in reach too, including the one you're clinging to. Gnash swings or shoots whatever he's holding.
- **Kick through a wall:** stomp while spider-climbing smashes a hole in the wall you're on and puts you inside, on the floor behind it. Into solid rock, it kicks out a hollow and you keep climbing, so you can tunnel in.
- **Goblins hit your vehicle:** a goblin that reaches Gus or Gertie while they're in a vehicle now clubs the vehicle instead of doing nothing, the same way its shots already did.

### World

- The pavement stairwells down to the sewers are gone. The sewers are still under every town street, but the only way in now is to smash through.
- Subway station stairs are built from real stair blocks, so the way down to the platforms is a smooth flight of steps instead of a staircase of plain blocks.

- The quick debug menu has a second tab, Show / hide (LB / RB or Tab), for choosing which switches the first tab lists, with Show all and Hide all at the top. Your choice is remembered.
- The debug switches are only in the quick debug menu now. Game options keeps just the switch that allows it, which also shows or hides the Dev tab.

## Version 0.1

Everything from the move to Claude Code on November 2, 2026, up to the first release on GitHub on November 5. Each entry describes where things ended up, so features that were reworked along the way appear once.

**Old saves reset.** World generation changed many times, so cities saved before this update start fresh.

### Highlights

- Gus can spider-climb and ride moving vehicles, including trains, boats, planes and the roller coaster.
- Trains, planes, boats, theme park rides and zoo animals can all be destroyed.
- Cultists, thieves, preppers and militia now defend their sites, and military bases scramble real tanks and helicopters.
- Tanks and helicopters are proper fighting machines, with machine guns, rockets, shells that reload, lock-on and an aim marker that shows where each shot will really land.
- Goblins chase with real routes: they climb, jump gaps, swim and use stairs and doors.
- Almost every building has been resized and furnished room by room, with stairs and bathrooms where they make sense, and outdoor sites are dressed with props.
- Goblin mode lets you drive anything, and the new Testing Environment spawns any structure, vehicle or enemy on demand.
- The game runs much faster, and can be played on the web.

### Enemies and goblins

**Cultists**

- Robed, hooded cultists now gather live at their altars and shuffle around them until disturbed.
- Come within 15 blocks of the altar or hurt one and the whole cult turns on you.
- They conjure fireballs at range and stab with ritual daggers up close.
- Killed cultists stay dead until the cult has had two quiet minutes.

**Compound guards**

- Thieves' hideouts, prepper bunkers and militia camps now have live guards who mill about until you come close or hurt one.
- Thieves stab with daggers.
- Preppers carry rifles and pipe bombs. Militia carry rifles and grenades.
- Tower guards hold their posts.

**Goblin pathfinding**

- Goblins now find real routes to Gus instead of falling apart in big buildings.
- They hop ledges and railings, jump gaps, climb walls in both directions, swim, and use stairs and doors.
- With a clear path, they run straight at where Gus is now rather than where he recently was.
- They leap at Gus when he's on top of a tall vehicle, and still find him when he's sunk.
- They walk around furniture, and shove it over if it holds them up.
- They no longer perch on stair rails, and they swim lower in the water.

### Combat, vehicles and destruction

**Military bases**

- Parked tanks and helicopters are now real vehicles. Entering a base or hitting one sounds the alert.
- Tanks hunt you and fire shells. Helicopters lift off, circle, and strafe with bursts and rockets.
- They're solid. Moving tanks run goblins down and ram cars.
- Destroyed armour stays destroyed in your save. The base stands down 30 seconds after you leave.

**Driving and vehicle combat**

- In goblin mode, press F (Y on a controller) to get in or out of cars, trucks, trains, boats, tanks, helicopters and planes.
- In a tank, bite fires a shell. In a helicopter, bite (left click or RB) fires a machine gun and stomp (right click or RT) fires rockets. In anything else, bite sounds the horn.
- Tank shells, the helicopter gun and rockets have magazines and reload times, and an ammo panel shows what's left, with a reload bar and countdown.
- A driving display shows the vehicle's health, its weapon and a crosshair.
- Lock on (Q, or R3 on a controller) to tanks and helicopters from your own, and the gun aims ahead of a moving target.
- The camera sits higher and further back in tanks and helicopters. The aim marker follows the real path of the shell or rocket and marks where it will go off: faint when it would never land on anything, and orange at the screen edge when the spot is off-screen.
- Tank shells and helicopter rockets blow bigger craters and damage vehicles, which jolt, smoke, burn and show health bars.
- Crashing aircraft blow craters, and stopped helicopters and wrecks drop when the ground under them goes.
- Driven cars climb one-block steps.
- Motorcycles join the traffic and can be ridden in goblin mode.

**Trains**

- Trains now have an engine at each end and reverse smoothly at the end of the line.
- Each car has its own health. Losing one stops the train and scores 500, and destroying them all scores 1000 more. Subway trains can be wrecked too.
- A moving train throws you forward and up instead of nudging you aside.

**Airplanes**

- Land on any airport plane and ride it through taxiing, takeoff, flight and landing.
- Planes have 300 health and score 1000 points. Shot down, one falls and blows a crater.
- A replacement arrives two minutes later, once no one's within 350 blocks.
- Moving planes strike goblins, cars and you. Parked planes no longer clip the terminals.

**Boats**

- Every boat is a new model: pedal boat, rowboat, speedboat, launch, yacht, trawler, tug, ferry, warship and container ship.
- All of them can be stood on, ridden and wrecked. They burn and list when hurt, sink when wrecked, and are replaced later.
- At sea they follow routes on open water. Others are moored at harbours, quays, dry docks, beaches and rigs.
- Jump while swimming beside a boat to climb aboard.

**Theme parks and zoos**

- Coaster cars, the Ferris wheel and the carousel can all be destroyed. Break the track and the coaster derails.
- Zoo animals can be killed. Wrecks and deaths are saved with your world.

**Traffic and crashes**

- Ordinary traffic now runs goblins down, not just police cars.
- Trains, boats and planes crash when they meet.
- A car hitting a wall faster than 13 blocks a second crushes a hole sized by its speed, takes heavy damage and carries on through. Slower bumps still bounce off.

**News crews**

- News crews now drive out from news stations to cover fires, car crashes and killings.

### Movement

- **Riding vehicles:** Drop on to any moving vehicle and you land on it unhurt, unless you fell from a real height. You're carried round bends and stay on the roof going down ramps.
- **Roller coaster:** Gus can now land on and ride coaster cars for whole laps.
- **Climbing vehicles:** Gus can cling to and climb cars, trucks, train cars, ship hulls, container stacks and planes while they move. Climb over the top to ride on the roof, or walk off the roof with climb held to tip on to the side. Hold climb as a vehicle hits you to grab on.
- **Spider climb:** Gus can now climb on to pillars and one-block-deep ledges. He grabs walls at a slant, and holding climb stops a sprint from smashing what he's about to climb.
- **Stairs and ramps:** Gus can walk up stairs. He stays on ramps going down instead of bouncing, and sprinting uphill no longer breaks the ramp's support.

### Buildings and interiors

**Bigger, more recognisable buildings**

- Cities and large towns are about 30% wider.
- Each kind of site has its place: offices downtown, hospitals in the ring, schools and superstores in the suburbs, heavy industry at the edge.
- Shops, restaurants, banks, libraries and discos get their own roof shapes, with bigger signs.
- Landmarks are easier to pick out, like the museum's copper dome, the whale on the aquarium and the train station clock tower.

**Site sizes, in plots**

- **Large:** Quarries are 6 x 6. Zoos are 5 x 5. Hospitals, universities, prisons, factories, studios and power stations run from 3 x 3 to 6 x 6.
- **Medium:** Bases and dumps are 4 x 4. Lumber camps are 4 x 3. Mines and schools are 3 x 3. Museums, aquariums, furniture stores and cathedrals run from 2 x 3 to 4 x 3. Police stations are 2 x 2 with a rooftop helipad.

**Rebuilt and new**

- **Gas stations:** A branded canopy over three pump islands, with a tall price sign.
- **Fire stations:** Red bay fronts, a taller hose tower and a flag.
- **Schools:** A new larger site with a bus lane, clock tower and playing field.

- **Courthouses:** New, with a metal detector and a full courtroom.
- **The capitol:** Rebuilt inside with a rotunda, Senate and House chambers, courtroom, statuary hall, library and more.

**Furnished rooms**

- Furniture is now made of small, goblin-sized models instead of whole blocks. Knock it over as you go.
- Restaurants, shops, stores, malls, homes, flats, offices, public buildings, hospitals, venues and factories have laid-out rooms.
- Museums have themed galleries, including Egypt, knights and fossils. Sound stages each shoot a different set.
- Street benches, litter bins and beach and dock furniture are now models too.

**Stairs and bathrooms**

- Every multi-storey building that can fit stairs now has them, drawn as real steps with handrails. They can be smashed.
- Buildings that were too cramped have been enlarged so stairs, bathrooms and doors all fit.
- Bathrooms now appear wherever they'd logically be, sized to the floor, with matching walls and swinging doors.
- The university library has a second staircase at its far end.

### Outdoor sites

- Farmer's markets, camps, campsites, the cult altar, building sites, nurseries, trailer parks, cemeteries, mines, quarries, lumber camps, prepper, militia and thieves' compounds, ruins, dumps, parks, plazas, courts, lighthouses, power stations, zoos, theme parks, stadiums, arenas, car parks, bunkers and wrecked cars are all dressed with props.

### World, roads and coast

**Roads and traffic**

- Ramps taper to street width and carry their road markings all the way down.

- Traffic flows better, with longer green lights, proper following and lanes that merge before end ramps. The worst queues dropped from 12 to 15 cars down to 7 to 10.
- Fixed a jam where long trucks froze at the foot of ramps.

**Coast**

- Beaches and cliffs now blend gradually, and the sea bed is smooth.
- Places by the water become coastal cities, harbor towns, resort towns or fishing villages. Shipping ports belong to coastal cities.
- The kind of place now shows under its name.

**Parody brands**

- About 40 more parody chains, nine new billboards, and about 90 chains with their own goblin take on a logo.

### Performance

- Toppling buildings and everyday frames are much cheaper. On an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER at default settings, standing still went from 207 to 379 fps and toppling from 133 to 278 fps, and the worst toppling frame fell from 189 ms to 18 ms.

- Goblin route maps take less time per frame while reaching further, and rescan less often. Smoke plumes only thin out when frames are running long.
