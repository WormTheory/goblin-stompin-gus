# Roads, traffic and shores: what changed

Each item is its own git commit, so anything can be undone on its own with `git revert`. Old saves reset because world generation changed (the save key went from v42 to v50).

## Results

Measured from generated worlds with the new audits in `tools/tests`. Ranges cover the worlds tested, using `SEED=1`, `2` and `3` unless noted.

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| Highways cutting through big buildings (`SEED=1`) | 12 | 0 |
| Structural deck dead ends and breaks | 0 | 0 |
| Worst traffic queue | 12 to 15 cars | 7 to 10 cars |
| Ramp samples with traffic stopped (`SEED=1`) | 53% | 20% |
| Straight shoreline cut-offs at block edges (`SEED=1`, `2`) | 25 and 29 | 9 and 12 |
| Sudden sea bed depth jumps (`SEED=1`, `2`) | 556 and 796 | 11 and 44 |

## Highways

- **Clipping:** highways leaving town cut straight through hospitals, cinemas and other big sites, because those sites were placed without checking for the routes. They now keep clear.
- **Dead ends:** the audit found no true structural dead ends. The abrupt-looking ones were motorways stopping just short of the town's other motorway. These now carry on over the crossing and get an interchange before ramping down.
- **Interchanges:** cloverleafs were city-only. Any place built up all round the crossing can now have one.
- **Ramps:** end ramps taper to street width instead of landing wider than the road.

## Traffic

- Motorway lanes merge into one well before an end ramp. Fighting over one street lane at the bottom was the main cause of the worst jams.
- Traffic lights now run a 24 second cycle with 10 seconds of green each way. It was 14 seconds with 5 of green.
- Cars follow at the speed of the car in front, instead of crawling behind anything nearby.
- Cars can now accelerate on ramps, where they could previously only coast.

## Markings

- Streets now have a solid double yellow centre line, matching the highways, and it runs on through plain bends.
- White stop lines mark lights and stop signs, and cars stop at them, behind the crossing.
- Parked cars are centred in their bays, and every bay has a closing line.

## Coast

- Beach width and cliffs blend gradually instead of switching at block edges.
- The sea bed follows the broad shape of the coast and shelves up to land, so small islands no longer stand on pillars.
- Dredged basins slope back up to the natural sea bed.
- Harbour roads only appear where a street actually reaches them.

## Parody

- About 40 more chains, including Goblin Home Depot, Taco Bell, KFC, Trader Joe's and Six Flags.
- Goblin versions of the logos for Wal-Mart, Target, McDonald's, Shell, Starbucks and Apple.
- Nine new billboards, such as "GOBLIN NIKE: Just stomp it."

## Also fixed

- Gus no longer stomps a pit by himself at startup.
- The test tools now run on Windows.
- The `stray` suite now reports whether a road block is actually cut off from the network, which is 0 in the results I checked.

## Still open

- **How it feels:** I checked numbers and screenshots, but I can't judge how driving feels or how smoothly it runs on your PC. Both need you to play it.
- **Crossings without an interchange:** a motorway crossing still gets none if the place isn't built up all round it. One of the three crossings in the `SEED=1` test area is like this.
- **Shoreline:** a few straight cut-offs remain, 3 to 12 per world.
- **Road edges:** barrier width changes on country highways show as small one-block jogs, and some side streets at the edge of town stop just short of the highway.
- **Logos:** a second pass gave about 85 chains their own parody logo. Cinemas, car dealers and casinos without a famous name still share generic ones.

## Tier 5: the largest sites
- Airport: 12 x 5 plots (was 6 x 2), with a second runway, a second terminal, cargo sheds, fuel tanks and more parking.
- Nuclear plant: moved out to the country at 6 x 6, with two reactors, turbine halls, cooling towers and a switchyard.
- Theme park: 6 x 6 walks and lawns, a far bigger coaster and wheel, more stalls, a haunted house, food hall and drop tower.
- Zoo: 5 x 5 with eight large enclosures.
- Shipping ports: longer stretches of quay, and more of them. Shore audit (seed 1) went from 9 to 14 edge cut-offs as a result.
- Mall: already 4 x 6, left as it was.

## Props in every ordinary building
- Shelves, fridges, sofas, beds, bunks, small tables, lone screens and plants are box-model props now, not blocks, in all the building kinds that share the furniture function. Malls, loose crates and auditorium seats are still blocks.
- Goblins walk round props, and shove one over if they're held up for more than a moment. Gus knocks them over as he walks through, for 5 points each.
- The collapse lean test now shows 0 straight falls and 12 none, where it was 7 and 5, with topples unchanged at 12. The loose furniture that used to fall is gone.

## Small restaurants
- One restaurant per block now, 16 x 13 from outside (it was two, 8 wide and 6 to 11 deep), with about twice as many blocks of them. The small car park behind them has gone to make room.
- Each has a dining room, a checkout counter with tills, and a kitchen behind a wall with a serving hatch and a lit menu. Diner, pizzeria and cafe each have their own furniture.
- The patio is props: umbrellas, tables and chairs.

## The other restaurants
- Fast food: one size, 14 x 13, with booths, tables, a counter with tills and a drinks machine, fryers and grills, and a drive-thru window. The small car park behind has gone.
- Sit-down places: at least 16 x 22 on their two-block sites, with a host stand, booths, tables, a bar with stools and bottles, a kitchen, and rows of real windows. Buffet and country-store chains keep their extras as props.
- Bars and soup kitchens: a block each instead of half of one, so a block of shops with one of those in it is now just that. Bars have a pool table, jukebox, taps and an outdoor patio.

## Small shops and big stores
- Bookshops, dentists, dispensaries and gun shops: one per block at 16 x 13, each with its own layout. There are about half as many shops per shop block, with about half again as many shop blocks.
- Supermarkets, drug stores, superstores and electronics stores: checkouts, trolleys, fruit and veg, signed aisles, a deli, a pharmacy, walls of TVs and pallet racking, depending on the store.
- The furniture store is a grid of furnished rooms with its cafeteria and warehouse. Malls have furnished shop units, a food court, kiosk counters and benches.
- Car dealers, armories and gas station stores use the same props, at their old sizes.

## Homes and offices
- Houses: two to a block, 16 x 8 (up to four before, about 7 x 7 each), or now and then one big one in the suburbs. Living room, kitchen, bedroom; upstairs bedrooms and a bathroom, with real stairs in two-storey houses.
- Flats: a corridor and up to four flats a floor, each with a bed, wardrobe, sofa and kitchenette.
- Offices: desk rows, filing, copier, coffee corner, a glass meeting room; lobbies have reception, lifts and seating. Lifts are for show.
- Banks, radio, news and the stock exchange have their own rooms.
- Towers and mid-rises now have stairs wherever they fit.
- `tour.js` takes a seventh scene value to slice the roof off and look down into a room.

## Public services and hospitals
- New courthouses in cities, large towns and small towns: a lobby with a metal detector, and a courtroom with a judge's bench, witness box, jury, lawyers' tables and public benches.
- Schools, libraries, police stations, fire stations and jails have laid-out rooms. One-block police posts now have stairs.
- Hospitals: upper floors now take turns as wards, private rooms and operating theatres, and big lobbies gain a cafe and gift shop.
- Dispatch unchanged: `dispatch.js` gives the same results before and after for fire engines, police cars and ambulances.

## Police and hospitals, second pass
- Police stations: a lobby with a front desk, then a hall with rooms either side: cell blocks with barred bays, armory, evidence room, break room, interview rooms, locker rooms and squad rooms, in a different order on each floor.
- Hospital ground floors: pharmacy, X-ray, exam rooms, lab, scanner room, supply room, staff room, admissions, chapel and gift shop; more seats, a cafe and an information desk in the lobby; the ER has resuscitation bays by the ambulance doors and many more treatment bays.
- Goblins find their way through doors between these rooms (`roomStep`).

## Culture, entertainment, industry
- Laid-out props in cinemas, theaters, discos, clubs, casinos, museums, aquariums, churches, cathedrals, factories, labs, barracks, airport terminals and film sound stages.
- Left mostly as they were: arena and stadium bowls, the capitol, data centers, lecture halls, mission control and turbine halls, whose blocks already read well.
- Fixed two bugs: rooms with very tall ceilings showed none of their props, and country buildings had grass for a floor.

## Stairs everywhere
- `autoStair` gives any building with a real upper storey a flight of stairs if it fits; `tools/tests/stairaudit.js` lists any without.
- Enlarged or reshaped to fit: armories (16 x 14), farmhouses and barns (side by side, no longer overlapping), mid-rises (at most two per block, car parks front or back), towers (tall lobbies and set-backs only where the stairs still fit), radio stations.
- The capitol has a way through from the rotunda to its wings, with stairs up the west wing only.
- No stairs on purpose: churches and cathedrals (one tall room), car parks (ramps), malls (escalators), lighthouses (their own spiral), hose towers (too narrow), buildings under construction.
